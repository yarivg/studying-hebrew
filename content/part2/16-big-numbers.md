# Numbers past a hundred, and the dual

Part I chapter 15 gave you one to a hundred in two genders, and warned you
that the feminine set is the one you count with. Past a hundred the system
gets easier rather than harder. There are two new words, *מֵאָה* and *אֶלֶף*,
and everything above them is those two words being counted by numbers you
already have.

## The hundreds

*מֵאָה* /me'a/ is a hundred. It is a feminine noun, and that single fact
decides the whole row.

::: rule
*מֵאָה* is feminine, so the hundreds are counted with the feminine numbers:
*שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת*, never *שְׁלוֹשָׁה מֵאוֹת*.
:::

| Number | Hebrew | |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | מֵאָה | |
| 200 | מָאתַיִים | a dual, not "two hundreds" |
| 300 | שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת | |
| 400 | אַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת | |
| 500 | חֲמֵשׁ מֵאוֹת | |
| 600 | שֵׁשׁ מֵאוֹת | |
| 700 | שְׁבַע מֵאוֹת | |
| 800 | שְׁמוֹנֶה מֵאוֹת | |
| 900 | תְּשַׁע מֵאוֹת | |

*מָאתַיִים* /matayim/ is the odd one out. It carries no number in front of it
and it never takes one, because the *ַיִים* on the end is the dual ending from
chapter 9 doing exactly what it does on *עֵינַיִים*: it means two of the
thing, and the thing here is a hundred. Two hundred is one word.

Compounds run from the largest piece down, with *וְ* before the last one.

::: examples
מֵאָה וְעֶשְׂרִים || 120
מָאתַיִים וַחֲמִישִּׁים || 250
שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת שִׁישִּׁים וְחָמֵשׁ || 365
תְּשַׁע מֵאוֹת תִּשְׁעִים וְתֵשַׁע || 999
:::

Only the last element has to agree with the noun being counted, so 102 books
is *מֵאָה וּשְׁנֵי סְפָרִים* and 102 women is *מֵאָה וּשְׁתֵּי נָשִׁים*. The
*מֵאָה* part does not move.

## The thousands

*אֶלֶף* /elef/ is a thousand and is masculine, plural *אֲלָפִים*. Masculine
numbers standing in front of a plural noun use their construct forms, which is
why the thousands look heavier than the hundreds.

| Number | Hebrew | |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | אֶלֶף | |
| 2,000 | אַלְפַּיִים | the dual again |
| 3,000 | שְׁלוֹשֶׁת אֲלָפִים | |
| 4,000 | אַרְבַּעַת אֲלָפִים | |
| 5,000 | חֲמֵשֶׁת אֲלָפִים | |
| 8,000 | שְׁמוֹנַת אֲלָפִים | |
| 10,000 | עֲשֶׂרֶת אֲלָפִים | |

::: warning
*שְׁתֵּי מֵאוֹת* and *שְׁנֵי אֲלָפִים* are both wrong. Two hundred and two
thousand each have one dedicated dual word, and the dual leaves no room for
the compound.
:::

Above that, Hebrew borrows: *מִילְיוֹן* /milyon/ a million, plural
*מִילְיוֹנִים*, and *מִילְיַארְד* /milyard/ a billion. Both are masculine and
both behave like ordinary nouns: *שְׁנֵי מִילְיוֹן* for two million, though
you will hear *שְׁנֵי מִילְיוֹנִים* just as often.

## Saying a year

A year is *שָׁנָה*, which is feminine, so a year is read out with the feminine
numbers, and *אֶלֶף* and *אַלְפַּיִים* lead the way they do anywhere else.

::: examples
אַלְפַּיִים עֶשְׂרִים וְחָמֵשׁ || 2025
אַלְפַּיִים וְשֵׁשׁ || 2006
אֶלֶף תְּשַׁע מֵאוֹת אַרְבָּעִים וּשְׁמוֹנֶה || 1948
בִּשְׁנַת אַלְפַּיִים || in the year 2000
:::

::: note
The Hebrew calendar does not use these at all. Its years are written with
letters standing for numbers, which is why you see *תשפ"ו* on a school
certificate rather than a digit. Chapter 15 covered the reading of those, and
chapter 20 comes back to the marks.
:::

## Prices

The currency is the *שֶׁקֶל* /shekel/, plural *שְׁקָלִים*, officially
*שֶׁקֶל חָדָשׁ*, and it is masculine. The sign is *₪*, written after the
number, and the abbreviation on a receipt is *ש"ח*. A hundred *אֲגוֹרוֹת*
/agorot/ make one shekel.

::: examples
כַּמָּה זֶה עוֹלֶה? || how much does this cost?
חֲמִישָּׁה שְׁקָלִים || five shekels
שֶׁקֶל וָחֵצִי || a shekel and a half
מֵאָה שֶׁקֶל || a hundred shekels
זֶה עוֹלֶה 250 ₪ || it costs 250 shekels
:::

::: tip
Listen to what Israelis actually do: below about ten they say
*חֲמִישָּׁה שְׁקָלִים* with the plural, and from ten upwards they switch to the
singular, *עֶשְׂרִים שֶׁקֶל*, *מֵאָה שֶׁקֶל*. It is the same habit English has
with "twenty quid". Using the plural throughout is not wrong; it just marks
you out.
:::

## The dual on time words

The dual is a closed set. You are never asked to invent one, and outside the
paired body parts it survives almost entirely on units of time.

| Dual | Meaning | Three of them |
|---|---|---|
| שָׁעָתַיִים | two hours | שָׁלוֹשׁ שָׁעוֹת |
| יוֹמַיִים | two days | שְׁלוֹשָׁה יָמִים |
| שְׁבוּעַיִים | two weeks | שְׁלוֹשָׁה שָׁבוּעוֹת |
| חוֹדְשַׁיִים | two months | שְׁלוֹשָׁה חוֹדָשִׁים |
| שְׁנָתַיִים | two years | שָׁלוֹשׁ שָׁנִים |
| פַּעֲמַיִים | twice | שָׁלוֹשׁ פְּעָמִים |

::: rule
The dual means exactly two, and it replaces the number entirely. At three the
word reverts to the ordinary plural with an ordinary number in front of it.
:::

::: warning
*שְׁנֵי יָמִים* and *שְׁתֵּי שָׁנִים* are the classic learner's slip. They are
understandable and they are not Hebrew. Two of these words is one word:
*יוֹמַיִים*, *שְׁנָתַיִים*.
:::

Not every time word plays. A minute has no dual: two minutes is
*שְׁתֵּי דַּקּוֹת* /dakot/, with a plain number. The list above is the list.

::: exercise Count it out
Say 300 || *שְׁלוֹשׁ מֵאוֹת* || *מֵאָה* is feminine, so the number is too
Say 200 || *מָאתַיִים* || one word, a dual, never *שְׁתֵּי מֵאוֹת*
Say 3,000 || *שְׁלוֹשֶׁת אֲלָפִים* || *אֶלֶף* is masculine and takes the construct number
Read the year 2025 || *אַלְפַּיִים עֶשְׂרִים וְחָמֵשׁ* || feminine, because *שָׁנָה* is
Say "a hundred shekels" || *מֵאָה שֶׁקֶל* || singular *שֶׁקֶל* from ten upwards in speech
Say "two weeks" || *שְׁבוּעַיִים* || the dual, with no number in front
Say "three weeks" || *שְׁלוֹשָׁה שָׁבוּעוֹת* || past two the ordinary plural comes back
Say "twice" || *פַּעֲמַיִים* || and three times is *שָׁלוֹשׁ פְּעָמִים*
:::
