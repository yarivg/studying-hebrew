# Prepositions that decline in the plural

You already know one set of preposition endings: *לִי*, *לְךָ*, *לָךְ*, *לוֹ*,
*לָהּ*, and the same set on *בְּ*, *שֶׁל*, *עִם*, *אֶצְלִי*. That is the first
declension, and it is the easy one. There is a second, and it takes the
endings that go on a **plural** noun, even though nothing about the meaning is
plural.

## The second set

Compare the two columns. The right one is the set you met on *סְפָרַיי* and
*סְפָרֶיךָ* in the last chapter.

| Person | *לְ* to | *עַל* /al/ on, about |
|---|---|---|
| me | *לִי* | *עָלַיי* |
| you m. | *לְךָ* | *עָלֶיךָ* |
| you f. | *לָךְ* | *עָלַיִיךְ* |
| him | *לוֹ* | *עָלָיו* |
| her | *לָהּ* | *עָלֶיהָ* |
| us | *לָנוּ* | *עָלֵינוּ* |
| you m.pl. | *לָכֶם* | *עֲלֵיכֶם* |
| you f.pl. | *לָכֶן* | *עֲלֵיכֶן* |
| them m. | *לָהֶם* | *עֲלֵיהֶם* |
| them f. | *לָהֶן* | *עֲלֵיהֶן* |

*עָלַיי* does not mean "on my things". It means "on me", one person. The plural
shape is a fossil: these prepositions were once nouns in the construct plural,
and the endings stuck.

::: examples
הַסֵּפֶר עָלָיו || the book is on him
שָׁמַעְתִּי עָלֶיהָ || I heard about her
עָלַיי לָלֶכֶת || I have to go
מַה דַּעְתְּךָ עָלֵינוּ || what do you think of us
:::

::: note
*עַל* carries two meanings at once: physical "on", and "about" after verbs of
speaking and hearing. *דִּיבַּרְנוּ עָלֶיךָ* is "we talked about you", not "we
talked on you". There is also a third use, *עָלַיי* plus an infinitive, meaning
"it falls to me to", which is formal but very common in signage and notices.
:::

## el, lifnei, acharei

The same endings, on three more prepositions you use constantly.

| Person | *אֶל* to, towards | *לִפְנֵי* /lifnei/ before | *אַחֲרֵי* /acharei/ after |
|---|---|---|---|
| me | *אֵלַיי* | *לְפָנַיי* | *אַחֲרַיי* |
| you m. | *אֵלֶיךָ* | *לְפָנֶיךָ* | *אַחֲרֶיךָ* |
| you f. | *אֵלַיִיךְ* | *לְפָנַיִיךְ* | *אַחֲרַיִיךְ* |
| him | *אֵלָיו* | *לְפָנָיו* | *אַחֲרָיו* |
| her | *אֵלֶיהָ* | *לְפָנֶיהָ* | *אַחֲרֶיהָ* |
| us | *אֵלֵינוּ* | *לְפָנֵינוּ* | *אַחֲרֵינוּ* |
| them m. | *אֲלֵיהֶם* | *לִפְנֵיהֶם* | *אַחֲרֵיהֶם* |

::: examples
פָּנִיתִי אֵלָיו || I turned to him
הוּא בָּא אֵלֵינוּ || he came to us
הִיא עָמְדָה לְפָנַיי בַּתּוֹר || she stood in front of me in the queue
כּוּלָּם הָלְכוּ אַחֲרָיו || everyone walked after him
יֵשׁ לָנוּ שָׁנָה שְׁלֵמָה לְפָנֵינוּ || we have a whole year ahead of us
:::

::: rule
If a preposition already ends in a vowel or already looks plural (*עַל*,
*אֶל*, *לִפְנֵי*, *אַחֲרֵי*, *תַּחַת*, *בֵּין*), it takes the plural endings. If
it is a single closed syllable (*לְ*, *בְּ*, *עִם*, *מִן*), it takes the
singular ones.
:::

The rule is a rule of thumb, not a law, and there are half a dozen
prepositions it does not cover. It is right often enough to guess with.

## The reading problem nobody warns you about

::: warning
*עָלָיו* and *עָלֶיהָ* are עליו and עליה unpointed. So are *אֵלָיו* and
*אֵלֶיהָ*: אליו and אליה. One letter apart, and the letter is a vav against a
he, which are not alike enough to catch at speed but not different enough to
be safe. You will misread this pair. Everybody does, and the fix is context,
not eyesight.
:::

It helps to know that the vav form is always masculine and the he form always
feminine, and that Hebrew mentions the subject nearby far more often than
English does. Read the sentence, not the word.

## bishvil

*בִּשְׁבִיל* /bishvil/, "for, for the sake of", belongs to the first
declension despite its length.

::: examples
בִּשְׁבִילִי || for me
בִּשְׁבִילְךָ || for you
בִּשְׁבִילוֹ || for him
קָנִיתִי אֶת זֶה בִּשְׁבִילָהּ || I bought this for her
:::

::: tip
*בִּשְׁבִיל* is purpose or benefit. *בִּגְלַל* /biglal/ is cause. "I came for
you" is *בָּאתִי בִּשְׁבִילְךָ*; "I came because of you" is *בָּאתִי
בִּגְלַלְךָ*. English "for" covers both, which is why this pair goes wrong.
:::

## min with a pronoun

*מִן* /min/ is the "from" that does the comparing, and its declension is
irregular enough to be worth its own list.

::: examples
מִמֶּנִּי || from me, than me
מִמְּךָ || from you, than you
מִמֶּנּוּ || from him, than him
מִמֶּנָּה || from her, than her
מֵאִיתָּנוּ || from us, than us
מֵהֶם || from them
:::

::: warning
*מִמֶּנּוּ* is both "from him" and, in formal Hebrew, "from us". Speech avoids
the clash by saying *מֵאִיתָּנוּ* for "from us" and leaving *מִמֶּנּוּ* to the
masculine singular.
:::

With the comparatives of chapter 9 this gives you the everyday sentence:

::: examples
הוּא גָּבוֹהַּ מִמֶּנִּי || he is taller than me
הִיא יוֹדַעַת יוֹתֵר מִמֶּנּוּ || she knows more than him
זֶה פָּחוֹת יָקָר מִזֶּה || this is less expensive than that
:::

::: exercise Attach the ending
Say "on me" || *עָלַיי* || plural endings, singular meaning
Say "about her" || *עָלֶיהָ* || *עַל* is also "about"
Say "he came to me" || *הוּא בָּא אֵלַיי* || *אֶל* takes the plural set
Say "in front of him" || *לְפָנָיו* || the same ending as *עָלָיו*
Why are עליו and עליה hard? || they differ by one letter and look alike unpointed || context decides, not the letter
Say "for you" (to a woman), meaning on your behalf || *בִּשְׁבִילֵךְ* || *בִּשְׁבִיל* uses the singular endings
Say "she is taller than me" || *הִיא גְּבוֹהָה מִמֶּנִּי* || *מִן* plus the ending, after the adjective
Which set does *עִם* take? || the singular set || *אִיתִּי*, *אִיתְּךָ*, from Part I
:::
