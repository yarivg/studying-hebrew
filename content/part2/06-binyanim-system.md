# The binyanim as a system

You have now conjugated verbs in five binyanim across three tenses without
ever being told what the binyanim are *for*. That was deliberate: the forms
have to be in your hands before the pattern behind them means anything. This
chapter is the pattern. Seven *בִּנְיָינִים* /binyanim/, one root poured into
each, seven related but different verbs out the other end.

## The seven at one glance

The traditional names are themselves the pattern, built on the dummy root
פ-ע-ל. *פָּעַל* /pa'al/ is the shape of *כָּתַב*; *הִפְעִיל* /hif'il/ is the
shape of *הִכְתִּיב*. Learn the name and you have learned the mould.

| Binyan | Past 3ms | What it does | Worked on כ-ת-ב |
|---|---|---|---|
| פָּעַל | כָּתַב | plain active | כָּתַב, he wrote |
| נִפְעַל | נִכְתַּב | passive or intransitive of pa'al | נִכְתַּב, it was written |
| פִּיעֵל | דִּיבֵּר | transitive, often intensive | כִּיתֵּב, he copied someone in |
| פּוּעַל | דּוּבַּר | passive of pi'el | כּוּתַּב, he was copied in |
| הִפְעִיל | הִכְתִּיב | causative | הִכְתִּיב, he dictated |
| הוּפְעַל | הוּכְתַּב | passive of hif'il | הוּכְתַּב, it was dictated |
| הִתְפַּעֵל | הִתְכַּתֵּב | reflexive or reciprocal | הִתְכַּתֵּב, he corresponded |

One root, כ-ת-ב, and seven verbs about writing: writing something, being
written, dictating, being dictated, writing to each other. Nothing in that
list had to be memorised as a separate word. The pi'el pair is the newest of
them: *כִּיתֵּב* is what Israelis say for copying someone in on an email.

::: rule
The root supplies the area of meaning. The binyan supplies the relationship
between the subject, the action and anyone else involved.
:::

## The pairs that make it legible

Two of the seven exist only to be the passive of another one. They are not
independent verbs; they are the same verb with the subject and object swapped.

::: examples
הַמּוֹרֶה דִּיבֵּר עַל הַסֶּרֶט || the teacher spoke about the film
דּוּבַּר עַל הַסֶּרֶט || the film was discussed
הוּא הִכְתִּיב מִכְתָּב || he dictated a letter
הַמִּכְתָּב הוּכְתַּב || the letter was dictated
:::

*נִפְעַל* /nif'al/ does the same job for *פָּעַל*, with one difference: it is a
working verb in its own right and half of its members are not passive at all.
*נִכְנַס* /nikhnas/, he entered, is nif'al and is as active as a verb gets.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| כָּתַב he wrote | נִכְתַּב it was written |
| דִּיבֵּר he spoke | דּוּבַּר it was spoken of |
| הִכְתִּיב he dictated | הוּכְתַּב it was dictated |

## The causative chain

*הִפְעִיל* /hif'il/ most often means causing someone else to do the pa'al verb.

::: examples
הוּא עָמַד || he stood
הוּא הֶעֱמִיד אֶת הַכִּיסֵּא || he stood the chair up
הוּא לָבַשׁ מְעִיל || he wore a coat
הוּא הִלְבִּישׁ אֶת הַיֶּלֶד || he dressed the child
:::

Sometimes the causative slot is filled by pi'el instead: *לָמַד* /lamad/ he
learned, *לִימֵּד* /limed/ he taught. There is no *הִלְמִיד*; the language
already had a word and did not need one. The principle holds, but which binyan
it is spent on varies from root to root.

## Where the system does not deliver

This is a strong tendency, not a rule, and pretending otherwise will mislead
you in week three.

::: warning
*סִיפֵּר* /siper/, he told, is pi'el and is not intensive anything.
*הִבְטִיחַ* /hivtiach/, he promised, is hif'il and causes nothing.
*הִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ* /hishtamesh/, he used, is hitpa'el and is neither reflexive nor
reciprocal; it takes *בְּ*: *הִשְׁתַּמַּשְׁתִּי בַּמַּחְשֵׁב*, I used the computer.
:::

Use the binyan to guess a meaning you do not know, then check it. Never use it
to overrule a dictionary.

::: note
In hitpa'el, a root beginning with שׂ שׁ ס ז swaps places with the ת of the
prefix: not *הִתְשַׁמֵּשׁ* but *הִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ*, not *הִתְסַדֵּר* but *הִסְתַּדֵּר*
/histader/, he managed. The ear does the swapping for you; the spelling just
records it.
:::

## The two with no infinitive

*פּוּעַל* /pu'al/ and *הוּפְעַל* /huf'al/ have a past, a present and a future,
and nothing else. No infinitive, no imperative, no verbal noun.

::: rule
You cannot intend, be asked, or be told to undergo something. An infinitive
exists to sit after *רוֹצֶה*, *צָרִיךְ* or *יָכוֹל*, and a purely passive form
has no doer to want anything.
:::

So "the letter has to be dictated" is not built from huf'al. Hebrew turns the
sentence around: *צָרִיךְ לְהַכְתִּיב אֶת הַמִּכְתָּב*, one has to dictate the
letter. Both passive binyanim are also rare in speech, which reaches for
*הֵם* plus an active verb instead. Part III comes back to them.

::: tip
To identify a binyan in the wild, look at the present tense, which is the most
distinctive: *כּוֹתֵב* is pa'al, *נִכְתָּב* is nif'al, *מְדַבֵּר* is pi'el,
*מְדוּבָּר* is pu'al, *מַכְתִּיב* is hif'il, *מוּכְתָּב* is huf'al,
*מִתְכַּתֵּב* is hitpa'el. Three of them begin with מ and a shuruk or a
kamatz-flavoured vowel: those are the passives.
:::

::: exercise Name the binyan
*הִסְבִּיר* || hif'il || the הִ prefix and the חִירִיק-yod inside
*הִתְלַבֵּשׁ* || hitpa'el || הִתְ plus a doubled middle letter
*נִכְנַס* || nif'al || active in meaning, nif'al in form
*דּוּבַּר* || pu'al || the shuruk after the first letter marks a passive
What is the passive of *דִּיבֵּר*? || *דּוּבַּר* || pi'el pairs with pu'al
What is the passive of *הִכְתִּיב*? || *הוּכְתַּב* || hif'il pairs with huf'al
Why is there no infinitive *לְדוּבַּר*? || pu'al has no infinitive || rephrase with an active verb instead
Turn *עָמַד* into "he stood something up" || *הֶעֱמִיד* || pa'al to hif'il is the causative step
:::
