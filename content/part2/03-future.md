# Future tense

The past put everything on the end of the verb. The future is the only tense
that works from both ends at once: a prefix says who, and a suffix, when there
is one, says what gender and number.

## The prefix carries the person

Four prefixes cover all six persons.

| Prefix | Person |
|---|---|
| *אֶ* | I |
| *תִּ* | you (all), and she |
| *יִ* | he, and they |
| *נִ* | we |

Two suffixes then split what the prefix left ambiguous: *ִי* for the feminine
singular "you", and *וּ* for any plural.

## לִכְתּוֹב in the future

| Pronoun | Future | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | אֶכְתּוֹב | I will write |
| אַתָּה | תִּכְתּוֹב | you will write (m) |
| אַתְּ | תִּכְתְּבִי | you will write (f) |
| הוּא | יִכְתּוֹב | he will write |
| הִיא | תִּכְתּוֹב | she will write |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | נִכְתּוֹב | we will write |
| אַתֶּם / אַתֶּן | תִּכְתְּבוּ | you will write (pl) |
| הֵם / הֵן | יִכְתְּבוּ | they will write |

Eight rows, seven distinct words. That is not a typo.

::: warning
*תִּכְתּוֹב* is both "you (m) will write" and "she will write". This is the one
genuine ambiguity in the Hebrew verb system, and it never goes away: it holds
in every binyan and every root. Context resolves it, and when context cannot,
Israelis put the pronoun in.
:::

::: note
The Academy also lists *תִּכְתּוֹבְנָה* for "you (f pl)" and for "they (f)".
Nobody says it. Nobody has said it in ordinary speech for a long time, and
saying it would sound like reciting the Bible at a bus stop. Recognise it in
older texts; use *תִּכְתְּבוּ* and *יִכְתְּבוּ*.
:::

## The same four prefixes, other binyanim

Only the stem after the prefix changes. Pi'el, *לְדַבֵּר* /ledaber/:

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| אֲנִי | אֲדַבֵּר |
| אַתָּה / הִיא | תְּדַבֵּר |
| אַתְּ | תְּדַבְּרִי |
| הוּא | יְדַבֵּר |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | נְדַבֵּר |
| הֵם / הֵן | יְדַבְּרוּ |

Hif'il, *לְהַסְבִּיר* /lehasbir/:

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| אֲנִי | אַסְבִּיר |
| אַתָּה / הִיא | תַּסְבִּיר |
| אַתְּ | תַּסְבִּירִי |
| הוּא | יַסְבִּיר |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | נַסְבִּיר |
| הֵם / הֵן | יַסְבִּירוּ |

::: tip
The vowel under the prefix is your binyan signal. Pa'al takes *i* (*תִּכְתּוֹב*),
pi'el takes a shva or a chataf (*תְּדַבֵּר*), hif'il takes *a* (*תַּסְבִּיר*).
The ה of *לְהַסְבִּיר* vanishes in the future exactly as it does in the present.
:::

::: examples
מָחָר אֶכְתּוֹב לָךְ || tomorrow I will write to you (f)
הִיא תְּדַבֵּר עִם הַמּוֹרֶה || she will speak to the teacher
נַסְבִּיר לָהֶם מָחָר || we will explain it to them tomorrow
הֵם לֹא יִכְתְּבוּ אֶת זֶה || they won't write it
:::

## The future is also the imperative

In writing, Hebrew has a separate command form. In speech, the ordinary future
does the job, and it is the polite way to do it.

::: examples
תִּכְתּוֹב לִי || write to me
תְּדַבְּרִי לְאַט || speak slowly (to a woman)
תִּסְגְּרוּ אֶת הַדֶּלֶת || close the door (to several people)
:::

Nothing is added and nothing is dropped; the same word means "you will write"
and "write". Tone and situation separate them. The real imperative, and when
it is worth using, is chapter 7.

::: rule
Prefix for the person, suffix for the gender and number. If a future form has
no prefix, it is not a future form.
:::

## What English speakers get wrong

There is no "will" to translate. English builds its future from two words, so
the instinct is to look for a Hebrew helper verb and put an infinitive after
it. There is no helper and no infinitive: *אֶכְתּוֹב* is one word doing all of
it. And *לִכְתּוֹב* after another verb is still only ever "to write".

::: exercise Build the future
I will write || *אֶכְתּוֹב* || *אֶ* is the first person prefix
they will write || *יִכְתְּבוּ* || *יִ* for the person, *וּ* for the plural
you (f) will write || *תִּכְתְּבִי* || the *ִי* is what marks the feminine
she will speak || *תְּדַבֵּר* || pi'el keeps its i-e stem, with a shva prefix
we will explain || *נַסְבִּיר* || hif'il prefixes carry an *a*
Which two people share *תִּכְתּוֹב*? || you (m singular) and she || the one real ambiguity in the system
Say "close the door" to a man || *תִּסְגּוֹר אֶת הַדֶּלֶת* || the plain future is the everyday imperative
Why is *תִּכְתּוֹבְנָה* not taught as the form to use? || it is the Academy's feminine plural and it is not used in speech || say *תִּכְתְּבוּ*
:::
