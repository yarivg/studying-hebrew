# Past tense: the other binyanim

Here is the good news, and it is better news than anything in the present
tense. The past endings you learned for *כָּתַבְתִּי* are the same endings in
every single binyan. *ְתִּי*, *ְתָּ*, *ְתְּ*, nothing, *ָה*, *ְנוּ*, *ְתֶּם*,
*ְתֶּן*, *וּ*, with no exceptions worth the name.

::: rule
Only the stem changes between binyanim in the past. The endings are identical
everywhere. Learn one set of nine endings and four stems, not four sets of
nine forms.
:::

## Pi'el: דִּיבֵּר

*לְדַבֵּר* /ledaber/, to speak. The stem is *דִּיבֵּר* /diber/: an *i* then an
*e*, and a dagesh sitting in the middle letter.

| Pronoun | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | דִּיבַּרְתִּי | I spoke |
| אַתָּה | דִּיבַּרְתָּ | you spoke (m) |
| הוּא | דִּיבֵּר | he spoke |
| הִיא | דִּיבְּרָה | she spoke |
| הֵם / הֵן | דִּיבְּרוּ | they spoke |

The *e* of *דִּיבֵּר* turns into an *a* the moment an ending is attached:
*דִּיבַּרְתִּי*, not *דִּיבֵּרְתִּי*. The same shift happens in the next two
binyanim, which is one fewer thing to remember.

## Hif'il: הִסְבִּיר

*לְהַסְבִּיר* /lehasbir/, to explain. The stem begins with *הִ* and has a long
*i* in the middle: *הִסְבִּיר* /hisbir/.

| Pronoun | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | הִסְבַּרְתִּי | I explained |
| אַתָּה | הִסְבַּרְתָּ | you explained (m) |
| הוּא | הִסְבִּיר | he explained |
| הִיא | הִסְבִּירָה | she explained |
| הֵם / הֵן | הִסְבִּירוּ | they explained |

Notice where the *i* survives. Before a vowel ending it stays: *הִסְבִּירָה*,
*הִסְבִּירוּ*. Before a consonant ending it collapses to *a*: *הִסְבַּרְתִּי*.

## Hitpa'el: הִתְלַבֵּשׁ

*לְהִתְלַבֵּשׁ* /lehitlabesh/, to get dressed. The stem opens with *הִתְ*,
which is as clear a marker as Hebrew ever gives you.

| Pronoun | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | הִתְלַבַּשְׁתִּי | I got dressed |
| אַתָּה | הִתְלַבַּשְׁתָּ | you got dressed (m) |
| הוּא | הִתְלַבֵּשׁ | he got dressed |
| הִיא | הִתְלַבְּשָׁה | she got dressed |
| הֵם / הֵן | הִתְלַבְּשׁוּ | they got dressed |

## Nif'al: נִכְנַס

This one is new. *נִפְעַל* /nif'al/ is the fourth binyan you need, and the
past is the easiest place to meet it: the stem simply starts with *נִ*.

| Pronoun | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | נִכְנַסְתִּי | I entered |
| אַתָּה | נִכְנַסְתָּ | you entered (m) |
| הוּא | נִכְנַס | he entered |
| הִיא | נִכְנְסָה | she entered |
| הֵם / הֵן | נִכְנְסוּ | they entered |

Nif'al is often the passive of pa'al: *כָּתַב* he wrote, *נִכְתַּב* it was
written. Just as often it is not passive at all, only intransitive: something
happens and nobody is named as doing it. Four very common ones:

::: examples
נִכְנַסְתִּי לַחֶדֶר || I went into the room
הַסֵּפֶר נִמְצָא עַל הַשּׁוּלְחָן || the book is on the table
נִשְׁאַרְתִּי בַּבַּיִת || I stayed at home
הַשִּׁיעוּר נִגְמַר || the lesson is over
:::

::: note
*נִמְצָא* /nimtza/ and *נִגְמַר* /nigmar/ are listed here as past forms and are
constantly used with a present meaning: "is located", "is finished". That is
not sloppiness, it is what a resulting state sounds like in Hebrew.
:::

## Telling them apart on the page

The past has one enormous advantage over the present: there is no *מ* prefix
anywhere. A verb starting with *מְ* or *מַ* or *מִתְ* is present tense, full
stop. So:

| If the verb | then it is |
|---|---|
| starts with *מ* | present, not past |
| has a dagesh in the middle letter and an i-e stem | pi'el |
| starts with *הִ* and has an *i* | hif'il |
| starts with *הִתְ* | hitpa'el |
| starts with *נִ* | nif'al |
| has none of the above, just three letters with a-a | pa'al |

::: warning
*הִסְבִּיר* and *הִתְלַבֵּשׁ* both open with ה, and beginners read one as the
other. The tell is the second letter: a ת immediately after the ה means
hitpa'el. Nothing else does.
:::

::: tip
Both ה binyanim lose their ה in the present (*מַסְבִּיר*, *מִתְלַבֵּשׁ*) and get
it back in the past. If you can see the ה, you are looking at something that
already happened.
:::

::: exercise Which binyan, and which person
*דִּיבַּרְתִּי* || pi'el, first person singular || the dagesh in the ב plus the *ְתִּי* ending
*הִסְבִּירָה* || hif'il, she || the *i* survives because the ending starts with a vowel
*הִתְלַבְּשׁוּ* || hitpa'el, they || *הִתְ* opens every hitpa'el past
*נִכְנַסְנוּ* || nif'al, we || the *נִ* is the binyan, the *ְנוּ* is the person
Is *מְדַבֵּר* past or present? || present || no past form in any binyan begins with מ
Say "she spoke" || *הִיא דִּיבְּרָה* || same *ָה* ending as *כָּתְבָה*
Say "we stayed" || *נִשְׁאַרְנוּ* || nif'al stem plus the ordinary ending
Why is there only one set of endings to learn? || the endings mark the person, and the person is independent of the binyan || the binyan lives in the stem
:::
