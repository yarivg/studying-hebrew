# Verbal nouns

Every binyan has one noun shape attached to it, and that shape names the act
of doing the verb. Hebrew calls it *שֵׁם הַפְּעוּלָה* /shem hape'ula/, the name
of the action. This is the best exchange rate in the language: learn one
shape, and every verb you already know in that binyan hands you a second word
for free.

## One shape per binyan

| Binyan | Shape | Verb | Verbal noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| pa'al | .ְ.ִי.ָה | כָּתַב | כְּתִיבָה, writing |
| pa'al | | קָרָא | קְרִיאָה, reading |
| pa'al | | שָׁתָה | שְׁתִייָּה, drinking |
| pa'al | | אָכַל | אֲכִילָה, eating |
| pi'el | .ִי.ּוּ. | דִּיבֵּר | דִּיבּוּר, speech |
| pi'el | | סִיפֵּר | סִיפּוּר, a story |
| pi'el | | טִיֵּיל | טִיּוּל, a trip |
| pi'el | | שִׁינָּה | שִׁינּוּי, a change |
| hif'il | הַ.ְ.ָ.ָה | הִסְבִּיר | הַסְבָּרָה, explaining |
| hif'il | | הֶחְלִיט | הַחְלָטָה, a decision |
| hif'il | | הִזְמִין | הַזְמָנָה, an invitation |
| hif'il | | הִתְחִיל | הַתְחָלָה, a beginning |
| hitpa'el | הִתְ.ַ.ְּ.וּת | הִתְלַבֵּשׁ | הִתְלַבְּשׁוּת, getting dressed |
| hitpa'el | | הִתְקַשֵּׁר | הִתְקַשְּׁרוּת, making contact |
| hitpa'el | | הִתְפַּתֵּחַ | הִתְפַּתְּחוּת, development |
| nif'al | הִי.ָּ.ְ.וּת | נִכְנַס | הִיכָּנְסוּת, entering |

::: rule
The root supplies the meaning, the shape supplies the fact that this is a
noun. You do not memorise verbal nouns one by one; you memorise five shapes.
:::

Notice how visible each shape is. Pa'al puts a *ִי* between the second and
third root letters and an *ָה* on the end. Pi'el keeps its own *ִי* after the
first letter and swaps the verb's vowel for a *וּ*. Hif'il trades the *הִ* of
the verb for a *הַ* and ends in *ָה*. Hitpa'el and nif'al both end in *וּת*,
which is the most recognisable ending of the five.

::: note
*פּוּעַל* and *הוּפְעַל* have no verbal noun, for the same reason they have no
infinitive: there is no doer. The five above are the whole system.
:::

## Reading one you have never met

This is the point of the table. When an unknown noun turns up, do two things:
strip the shape off and look at what letters are left.

::: examples
הַגְדָּלָה || הַ.ְ.ָ.ָה on ג-ד-ל, big: an enlargement
הִתְרַגְּשׁוּת || הִתְ.ַ.ְּ.וּת on ר-ג-שׁ, feeling: excitement
בְּדִיקָה || .ְ.ִי.ָה on ב-ד-ק, check: a check, a test
חִיפּוּשׂ || .ִי.ּוּ. on ח-פ-שׂ, search: a search
הַפְסָקָה || הַ.ְ.ָ.ָה on פ-ס-ק, stop: a break, an interval
:::

None of those had to be in a dictionary for you to get within a word of the
meaning. The shape also tells you which binyan the verb is in, which tells you
how to conjugate the verb once you go looking for it.

::: tip
The *וּת* ending is worth a reflex of its own. A noun ending in *וּת* is
almost always abstract and almost always feminine, whether it is a verbal noun
or not: *תַּרְבּוּת* /tarbut/ culture, *חֵירוּת* /kherut/ freedom.
:::

## It is a noun, all the way

A verbal noun is not a half-verb. It has a gender, it takes an article, it
takes adjectives, it goes into smikhut, and most of them have a plural.

::: examples
הַהַחְלָטָה הַזֹּאת || this decision
הַחְלָטוֹת קָשׁוֹת || difficult decisions
הַסִּיפּוּרִים שֶׁלּוֹ || his stories
שִׁינּוּי גָּדוֹל || a big change
שִׁיעוּרֵי הַנְּהִיגָה || the driving lessons
:::

The *ָה* shapes are feminine; the *.ִי.ּוּ.* shapes are masculine, with the
plural *ִים*; the *וּת* shapes are feminine and rarely pluralised at all.

## Where English would use a clause

This is the habit worth copying. Where English happily hangs a whole clause
off a preposition, Hebrew reaches for the noun instead, because a Hebrew
preposition wants a noun after it.

| English | Hebrew |
|---|---|
| after reading the book | *אַחֲרֵי הַקְּרִיאָה בַּסֵּפֶר* |
| before the start | *לִפְנֵי הַהַתְחָלָה* |
| during the trip | *בִּזְמַן הַטִּיּוּל* |
| without an explanation | *בְּלִי הַסְבָּרָה* |
| I am against smoking | *אֲנִי נֶגֶד עִישּׁוּן* |

::: rule
Preposition plus verbal noun is the default way to say when, why and how. The
clause versions exist, and Part III takes them up, but Hebrew reaches for the
noun first.
:::

## Where the shape has drifted

A shape is a good bet, not a guarantee. Plenty of verbal nouns have settled
into one concrete meaning and stopped being about the action.

::: examples
סִיפּוּר || a story, the thing told, more than the telling
הַזְמָנָה || an invitation card, or an order in a restaurant
הַרְצָאָה || a lecture, an event with a room and a time
:::

::: warning
And some nouns simply look like verbal nouns and are not. *מִשְׂרָד* /misrad/,
an office, sits on ש-ר-ת but it is a *מִשְׁקָל* noun of the *מִ.ְ.ָ.* family,
not a verbal noun of *שֵׁירֵת* /sheret/. The verbal noun of *שֵׁירֵת* is
*שֵׁירוּת* /sherut/, service. When the shape does not match the table exactly,
it is not a verbal noun.
:::

::: exercise Build and read
Verbal noun of *כָּתַב* || *כְּתִיבָה* || pa'al: *.ְ.ִי.ָה*
Verbal noun of *דִּיבֵּר* || *דִּיבּוּר* || pi'el: *.ִי.ּוּ.*
Verbal noun of *הִתְחִיל* || *הַתְחָלָה* || hif'il swaps *הִ* for *הַ*
Verbal noun of *הִתְלַבֵּשׁ* || *הִתְלַבְּשׁוּת* || hitpa'el ends in *וּת*
*הַגְדָּלָה* is built on which root and binyan? || ג-ד-ל, hif'il || the shape *הַ.ְ.ָ.ָה* gives the binyan away
What does *שְׁתִייָּה* mean? || drinking, or a drink || pa'al of *שָׁתָה*
Say "after the beginning" || *אַחֲרֵי הַהַתְחָלָה* || a preposition takes a noun, not a clause
Why is *מִשְׂרָד* not a verbal noun? || it is a *מִ.ְ.ָ.* mishkal, not one of the five shapes || the verbal noun on that root is *שֵׁירוּת*
:::
