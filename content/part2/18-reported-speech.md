# Reporting what someone said

Reporting speech in Hebrew is almost embarrassingly easy, and the reason it
trips English speakers up is that English does something extra that Hebrew
does not. You have to learn to stop doing it.

## Hebrew does not shift the tense

English pulls a reported verb one step back into the past. "I am coming"
becomes "he said he **was** coming". Hebrew leaves the verb exactly as the
speaker said it.

::: rule
Report the tense the speaker actually used. *אָמַר שֶׁ* /amar she/ plus the
clause, unchanged. The only thing that moves is the pronouns.
:::

| English | Hebrew | Hebrew, literally |
|---|---|---|
| he said he was coming | *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא בָּא* | he said that he is coming |
| she said she was tired | *הִיא אָמְרָה שֶׁהִיא עֲיֵיפָה* | she said that she is tired |
| I thought you lived in Haifa | *חָשַׁבְתִּי שֶׁאַתָּה גָּר בְּחֵיפָה* | I thought that you live in Haifa |
| he said he would call | *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא יִתְקַשֵּׁר* | he said that he will call |
| she said she had already eaten | *הִיא אָמְרָה שֶׁהִיא כְּבָר אָכְלָה* | she said that she already ate |

Look at the last two. The future stays future and the past stays past, so the
rule is not "always use the present". The tense is measured from the moment of
speaking, not from the moment of reporting, and Hebrew simply keeps whatever
was true then.

::: warning
*הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא הָיָה בָּא* is not "he said he was coming". It is a
conditional, the *הָיָה* plus present of chapter 14, and it means something
closer to "he said he would have come". Reaching for a past tense here changes
the meaning rather than politely backdating it.
:::

::: tip
English can drop "that": "he said he was coming". Hebrew cannot drop *שֶׁ*.
Every reported clause is stitched on with it, with no space after it, because
it is a prefix and not a word.
:::

## The verbs that introduce a clause

All of them take the same *שֶׁ*, and all of them behave the same way.

::: examples
הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהַסֶּרֶט טוֹב || he said the film was good
הִיא סִיפְּרָה שֶׁהִיא גָּרָה בִּירוּשָׁלַיִם || she said she lived in Jerusalem
חָשַׁבְתִּי שֶׁאַתְּ בָּעֲבוֹדָה || I thought you were at work
לֹא יָדַעְתִּי שֶׁיֵּשׁ לְךָ אָח || I did not know you had a brother
הֵם הֶחְלִיטוּ שֶׁהֵם נוֹסְעִים || they decided they were travelling
הוּא הִבְטִיחַ שֶׁהוּא יַעֲזוֹר || he promised he would help
:::

*אָמַר* /amar/ and *סִיפֵּר* /siper/ divide the ground English covers with
"said" and "told": *אָמַר* is the plain one, *סִיפֵּר* is telling at some
length. Both take *לְ* for the listener: *אָמַרְתִּי לוֹ*, *סִיפְּרָה לִי*.

## Reported questions

A question loses its question mark and its rising tone, and gains a joining
word. Which one depends on the question.

::: rule
Yes or no question: *שָׁאַל אִם* /sha'al im/. Any other question: *שָׁאַל*
plus the question word, with no *אִם* at all.
:::

::: examples
הוּא שָׁאַל אִם אֲנִי בָּא || he asked if I was coming
הִיא שָׁאֲלָה אִם יֵשׁ חָלָב || she asked whether there was milk
הוּא שָׁאַל מָתַי אֲנִי בָּא || he asked when I was coming
הִיא שָׁאֲלָה אֵיפֹה הַמַּפְתֵּחַ || she asked where the key was
שָׁאַלְתִּי אוֹתוֹ לָמָּה הוּא לֹא בָּא || I asked him why he did not come
:::

The tense rule holds here too: *אֲנִי בָּא* in the original stays *אֲנִי בָּא*
in the report.

::: warning
*הַאִם*, the formal marker for a direct yes/no question from chapter 14,
becomes plain *אִם* when the question is reported. And *אִם* is not *עִם*,
with. They sound close and they are spelled differently: *אִם* with an alef,
*עִם* with an ayin.
:::

::: note
Hebrew never inverts a subject and verb to make a question, so a reported
question needs no untangling. *אֵיפֹה הַמַּפְתֵּחַ* is the direct question and
the reported one, word for word.
:::

## What does move: the pronouns

The one thing Hebrew does change is the person, exactly as English does, and
for the same reason: the speaker and the reporter are different people.

| Said | Reported |
|---|---|
| *אֲנִי בָּא* | *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא בָּא* |
| *הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי* | *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלּוֹ* |
| *אַתָּה צוֹדֵק* | *הִיא אָמְרָה שֶׁאֲנִי צוֹדֵק* |
| *רָאִינוּ אוֹתְךָ* | *הֵם אָמְרוּ שֶׁהֵם רָאוּ אוֹתִי* |

Verb endings carry person too, so the shift usually lands on the verb as well
as on the pronoun. *כָּתַבְתִּי* said by her is *הִיא אָמְרָה שֶׁהִיא כָּתְבָה*.

## Direct speech on the page

When the words are quoted rather than reported, Hebrew puts a colon before
them and wraps them in quotation marks.

::: examples
הוּא אָמַר: "אֲנִי בָּא מָחָר". || he said, "I am coming tomorrow."
הִיא שָׁאֲלָה: "אֵיפֹה הַמַּפְתֵּחַ?" || she asked, "Where is the key?"
:::

The mark itself is the *גֵּרְשַׁיִים* /gershayim/, the double stroke ״. It is
the same shape at both ends, with no curly opening and closing pair, and a
Hebrew keyboard produces the plain typewriter quote for it. The same mark
turns up inside abbreviations, which is chapter 20's problem.

::: exercise Report it
"אֲנִי עָיֵיף" reported by you about him || *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא עָיֵיף* || present stays present
"I thought she was at home" || *חָשַׁבְתִּי שֶׁהִיא בַּבַּיִת* || no backshift, and *שֶׁ* cannot be dropped
"He said he would come" || *הוּא אָמַר שֶׁהוּא יָבוֹא* || future in the original, future in the report
"She asked if I was coming" || *הִיא שָׁאֲלָה אִם אֲנִי בָּא* || yes or no question, so *אִם*
"He asked where I lived" || *הוּא שָׁאַל אֵיפֹה אֲנִי גָּר* || a question word takes no *אִם*
Which is wrong: *שָׁאַל אִם מָתַי* or *שָׁאַל מָתַי*? || *שָׁאַל אִם מָתַי* || never both joiners at once
She said to you "אַתָּה צוֹדֵק". Report it. || *הִיא אָמְרָה שֶׁאֲנִי צוֹדֵק* || the pronoun shifts even though the tense does not
What is wrong with *אָמַר הוּא בָּא*? || the *שֶׁ* is missing || English drops "that", Hebrew never drops *שֶׁ*
:::
