# Reading a headline

A newspaper headline is usually the first piece of real Hebrew a learner tries
to read, and it is close to the hardest thing in the paper. The article under
it is easier. This chapter is about why, and what to do.

Four things are working against you at once.

## No vowels

Everything in Part I chapter 7 applies, with none of the props. There is no
nikud in a newspaper, no picture, and no sentence before it to set the
context. You are guessing the vowels from the root and the pattern, and a
headline gives you the fewest words to guess from.

::: tip
Read the headline out loud, wrongly, and keep going. A wrong vowel rarely
blocks comprehension; stopping does. Israelis misread headlines too and fix
themselves at the next word.
:::

## No verb

Headlines are written as noun phrases. The verb that would link them is simply
left out, exactly as English does in "Minister in Washington".

::: examples
שַׂר הַבִּיטָּחוֹן בִּפְגִישָׁה עִם רֹאשׁ הַמֶּמְשָׁלָה || the defence minister in a meeting with the prime minister
מַשְׁבֵּר בְּמַעֲרֶכֶת הַחִינּוּךְ || a crisis in the education system
הַפְגָּנָה גְּדוֹלָה בְּתֵל אָבִיב || a large demonstration in Tel Aviv
:::

::: rule
A colon in a headline means "said". *צַהַ"ל: אֵין נִפְגָּעִים* is the army
saying there are no casualties, not a list.
:::

Where a verb does appear, it is usually past tense for what happened and
present tense for what is about to happen. *רֹאשׁ הַמֶּמְשָׁלָה יוֹצֵא
לְאֵירוֹפָּה* is not leaving now; he leaves tomorrow.

## Smikhut stacked deep

Chapter 25 of Part I gave you two nouns joined into one. Headlines chain three
and four, because a smikhut chain is the shortest way to say anything.

::: examples
שַׂר הַבִּיטָּחוֹן || the minister of defence
רֹאשׁ הַמֶּמְשָׁלָה || the head of the government, the prime minister
רֹאשׁ מֶמְשֶׁלֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל || the head of the government of Israel
וַעֲדַת הַחוּץ וְהַבִּיטָּחוֹן || the foreign affairs and defence committee
:::

::: rule
In a chain, only the last noun takes *הַ*, and every noun before it is in the
construct form. Read a chain from the right and translate it from the left:
*רֹאשׁ מֶמְשֶׁלֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל* is head, of the government, of Israel.
:::

## Abbreviations

Hebrew abbreviates constantly, and the marker is the *גֵּרְשַׁיִים*
/gershayim/, the double stroke ״, placed before the last letter. A single
stroke ׳, the *גֵּרֶשׁ* /geresh/, marks a word cut short instead. Israelis
read these at a glance and mostly as words, not as letters.

| Short | Stands for | Read as | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| צה"ל | צְבָא הַהֲגָנָה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל | צַהַ"ל | the Israeli army |
| ארה"ב | אַרְצוֹת הַבְּרִית | אַרְהַ"ב | the United States |
| רה"מ | רֹאשׁ הַמֶּמְשָׁלָה | in full | the prime minister |
| ח"כ | חֲבֵר כְּנֶסֶת | חַ"כּ | member of Knesset |
| בג"ץ | בֵּית הַמִּשְׁפָּט הַגָּבוֹהַּ לְצֶדֶק | בַּגָּ"ץ | the High Court |
| מנכ"ל | מְנַהֵל כְּלָלִי | מַנְכָּ"ל | chief executive |
| עו"ד | עוֹרֵךְ דִּין | in full | a lawyer |
| דו"ח | דִּין וְחֶשְׁבּוֹן | דּוּ"חַ | a report, or a traffic fine |
| ביה"ס | בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר | in full | the school |
| וכו' | וְכוּלֵּי | וְכוּלֵּי | and so on |

::: warning
Do not invent them. *ח"כ* is the abbreviation for a member of Knesset; *חכ"ם*
is a different word entirely and means wise. The gershayim sits before the
last letter, and moving it changes the word.
:::

::: note
The same mark does duty on a keyboard as the plain typewriter quote, so what
you type and what a typesetter sets look slightly different and mean the same
thing.
:::

## The words the news runs on

| Hebrew | | Hebrew | |
|---|---|---|---|
| מֶמְשָׁלָה | government | מִשְׁטָרָה | police |
| שַׂר | minister | פִּיגּוּעַ | a terror attack |
| כְּנֶסֶת | the Knesset | הַפְגָּנָה | a demonstration |
| בְּחִירוֹת | elections | מַשְׁבֵּר | a crisis |
| הֶסְכֵּם | an agreement | דּוּ"חַ | a report |

*מֶמְשָׁלָה* /memshala/, *מִשְׁטָרָה* /mishtara/, *הַפְגָּנָה* /hafgana/ and
*מַשְׁבֵּר* /mashber/ are all mishkal nouns of chapter 5, and *הַפְגָּנָה* is
a hif'il verbal noun of chapter 17. Twenty words of this list will unlock most
front pages.

## Three headlines, word by word

**שר הביטחון בפגישה עם ראש ממשלת ישראל**

*שַׂר* /sar/ minister, in construct; *הַבִּיטָּחוֹן* /habitachon/ the defence,
so together the defence minister. *בִּפְגִישָׁה* is *בְּ* plus *פְּגִישָׁה*
/pgisha/ a meeting, and the dagesh drops out of the *פ* when *בְּ* lands on
it. Then *עִם*, with, and a three-deep chain. No verb, and none needed.

**דו"ח חדש: משבר במערכת החינוך**

*דּוּ"חַ* a report, *חָדָשׁ* new, which tells you *דּוּ"חַ* is masculine. Then
the colon, meaning the report says: *מַשְׁבֵּר* a crisis,
*בְּמַעֲרֶכֶת הַחִינּוּךְ* in the system of education, with *בְּ* fused onto
the construct form *מַעֲרֶכֶת*.

**הכנסת אישרה את ההסכם עם ארה"ב**

This one has a verb. *אִישְּׁרָה* /ishra/ is pi'el past, third person feminine
singular, from א-ש-ר, to approve, and it is feminine because *כְּנֶסֶת* is.
Then *אֶת*, which tells you *הַהֶסְכֵּם* is the object before you have parsed
anything else, and *עִם אַרְהַ"ב*.

::: rule
Read the headline, decide what you think the story is, then read the first
sentence of the article to check. The first sentence is a full sentence with
verbs in it, and it will confirm or correct you in five seconds.
:::

::: exercise Decode it
What does a colon in a headline mean? || said || the name before it is the source
What is *צה"ל*? || *צְבָא הַהֲגָנָה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל*, the army || read as one word, *צַהַ"ל*
What is *ארה"ב*? || *אַרְצוֹת הַבְּרִית*, the United States || literally the states of the covenant
Which noun in *ראש ממשלת ישראל* carries the *הַ*? || none of them || the chain is made definite by *יִשְׂרָאֵל* being a name
Why is there no verb in *משבר במערכת החינוך*? || headlines drop the linking verb || the noun phrase is the whole headline
What is *דו"ח*? || *דִּין וְחֶשְׁבּוֹן*, a report || also the word for a traffic fine
Which binyan is *אישרה*? || pi'el || past, third person feminine singular, root א-ש-ר
What should you read after the headline? || the first sentence of the article || it has the verbs the headline dropped
:::
