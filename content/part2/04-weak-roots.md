# Weak roots

Some letters cannot do what the verb pattern asks of them. א ה ח ע ר refuse a
dagesh. ו and י collapse into the vowel next to them instead of standing as
consonants. נ at the start of a syllable gets swallowed by whatever follows.
A root containing one of these is called weak, and the textbooks label the
families by position: פ for the first letter of the root, ע for the second, ל
for the third, after פ-ע-ל itself.

::: rule
Nothing in this chapter is irregular. It is the same pattern you already know,
coping with a letter that cannot hold the position the pattern puts it in.
:::

## ל"ה: the third letter that is not there

The largest family, and the one with the most common verbs in it.

| Infinitive | Present | Past (he) | Past (I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| לִרְאוֹת /lirot/ to see | רוֹאֶה | רָאָה | רָאִיתִי |
| לִקְנוֹת /liknot/ to buy | קוֹנֶה | קָנָה | קָנִיתִי |
| לִרְצוֹת /lirtzot/ to want | רוֹצֶה | רָצָה | רָצִיתִי |

Look at *רָאִיתִי*: there is no ה in it at all. There never was. The historic
third letter of these roots is י, and the ה you see in *רָאָה* is a vowel
letter marking the final *a*, not a consonant. Once an ending is attached the
ה has nothing to do and leaves.

::: tip
Every ל"ה verb ends in *ֶה* in the masculine present and in *ִיתִי* in the
first person past. Two shapes, an enormous number of verbs.
:::

## פ"נ: the נ that dissolves

| Infinitive | Present | Past (he) | Future (I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| לִנְסוֹעַ /linsoa/ to travel | נוֹסֵעַ | נָסַע | אֶסַּע |
| לָקַחַת /lakachat/ to take | לוֹקֵחַ | לָקַח | אֶקַּח |

In the past and present the נ behaves. In the future it would have to sit at
the end of a closed syllable with no vowel of its own, and instead of doing
that it assimilates forward into the next letter and reappears as a dagesh:
*אֶסַּע*, not *אֶנְסַע*. The dot inside the ס is the missing נ.

*לָקַחַת* is not a פ"נ verb at all, its root is ל-ק-ח, but the ל behaves
exactly like a פ"נ נ. It is the only verb that does this, and it is one you
will use daily.

## ע"ו and ע"י: two-letter verbs

| Infinitive | Present | Past (I) | Future (I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| לָקוּם /lakum/ to get up | קָם | קַמְתִּי | אָקוּם |
| לָגוּר /lagur/ to live, reside | גָּר | גַּרְתִּי | אָגוּר |
| לָשִׁיר /lashir/ to sing | שָׁר | שַׁרְתִּי | אָשִׁיר |

The middle letter is ו or י and it is never a consonant. Only two consonants
ever surface, which is why these verbs are so short. The present has no
*וֹ..ֵ* shape here: *קָם* is the present, and the past of "he got up" is also
*קָם*.

::: warning
*הוּא קָם* is "he gets up" and "he got up". Both. The only way to tell them
apart is a time word: *הוּא קָם בְּשֶׁבַע* every morning, *הוּא קָם אֶתְמוֹל* is
yesterday. The other persons separate cleanly (*קַמְתִּי* is unambiguously past),
so it is only the third person that catches you.
:::

## פ"י: the yod that turns into a vowel

| Infinitive | Present | Past (I) | Future (I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| לָשֶׁבֶת /lashevet/ to sit | יוֹשֵׁב | יָשַׁבְתִּי | אֵשֵׁב |
| לָרֶדֶת /laredet/ to go down | יוֹרֵד | יָרַדְתִּי | אֵרֵד |
| לָלֶכֶת /lalekhet/ to go | הוֹלֵךְ | הָלַכְתִּי | אֵלֵךְ |

The initial י shows up as a consonant in the past and inside *יוֹ* in the
present, and in the future and the infinitive it disappears into a long vowel:
*אֵשֵׁב*, *לָשֶׁבֶת*. *לָלֶכֶת* joins the family by behaving like it, not by
having a י.

## The gutturals: א ה ח ע ר

These refuse two things: a dagesh, and a plain shva. Where the pattern wants a
shva they take a chataf vowel instead, and where it wants a dagesh they simply
lengthen the vowel before them.

| Infinitive | Present | Past (I) |
|---|---|---|
| לַעֲבוֹד /la'avod/ to work | עוֹבֵד | עָבַדְתִּי |
| לֶאֱכוֹל /le'ekhol/ to eat | אוֹכֵל | אָכַלְתִּי |
| לִשְׁמוֹעַ /lishmoa/ to hear | שׁוֹמֵעַ | שָׁמַעְתִּי |

*לַעֲבוֹד* is what *לִכְתּוֹב* becomes when the first root letter is ע: the
shva cannot stand, so it becomes a chataf patach, and the *i* in front of it
becomes an *a* to match.

::: sound
A word ending in ח or ע takes a patach under that letter which is pronounced
**before** it, not after: *שׁוֹמֵעַ* is /shomea/, *נוֹסֵעַ* is /nosea/. Read it
as "shome-a", never "shoma".
:::

::: exercise Name the weakness
Why is there no ה in *רָאִיתִי*? || the root's third letter is י, and the ה in *רָאָה* is only a vowel letter || every ל"ה verb behaves this way
Where did the נ of *לִנְסוֹעַ* go in *אֶסַּע*? || it assimilated into the ס and shows up as the dagesh || the same happens in *אֶקַּח*
Past or present: *הוּא גָּר בְּתֵל אָבִיב* || either, from the form alone || ע"ו verbs have the same third person in both
Say "I got up" || *קַמְתִּי* || only two consonants ever appear
Say "I will sit" || *אֵשֵׁב* || the פ"י yod dissolves into the prefix vowel
Why is it *לַעֲבוֹד* and not *לִעְבוֹד*? || ע cannot take a plain shva, so it takes a chataf patach || and the prefix vowel shifts to *a*
How is *שׁוֹמֵעַ* pronounced? || /shomea/, with the *a* before the ע || the same for *נוֹסֵעַ* and *יוֹדֵעַ*
:::
