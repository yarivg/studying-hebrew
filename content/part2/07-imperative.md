# Commands and requests

Hebrew has a real imperative, with its own forms, and Israelis mostly do not
use it. That sentence is the whole chapter in miniature: you need the written
imperative to read a sign, and you need the future tense to sound like a
person. This chapter gives you both, and the line between them.

## The written imperative

It is built by taking the future and knocking the prefix off. Nothing else
changes except the vowels that have to shift once the prefix is gone.

| Future | Imperative | |
|---|---|---|
| תִּכְתּוֹב | כְּתוֹב | write! (m) |
| תִּכְתְּבִי | כִּתְבִי | write! (f) |
| תִּכְתְּבוּ | כִּתְבוּ | write! (pl) |
| תֵּשֵׁב | שֵׁב | sit! (m) |
| תֵּשְׁבִי | שְׁבִי | sit! (f) |
| תֵּשְׁבוּ | שְׁבוּ | sit! (pl) |
| תְּדַבֵּר | דַּבֵּר | speak! (m) |
| תְּדַבְּרִי | דַּבְּרִי | speak! (f) |
| תְּדַבְּרוּ | דַּבְּרוּ | speak! (pl) |

Three forms only: you, feminine you, and plural. There is no imperative for
*הוּא* or *אֲנִי*, because you cannot order somebody who is not in the room.

::: examples
סְגוֹר אֶת הַדֶּלֶת || close the door
שְׁמַע || listen (m)
תֵּן לִי || give me (m)
קַח || take (m)
לֵךְ || go (m)
בּוֹאִי || come (f)
הַסְבֵּר || explain (m), from hif'il
הִתְלַבְּשִׁי || get dressed (f), from hitpa'el
:::

In hif'il and hitpa'el the prefix does not disappear, it changes: *תַּסְבִּיר*
gives *הַסְבֵּר* /hasber/, *תִּתְלַבֵּשׁ* gives *הִתְלַבֵּשׁ*.

## What Israelis actually say

The future tense, used as it stands, is the ordinary spoken command.

::: examples
תִּכְתּוֹב לִי || write to me
תָּבוֹא הֵנָּה || come here
תַּגִּיד || tell me, say
תֵּשֵׁב בְּבַקָּשָׁה || sit down please
תְּנִי לִי רֶגַע || give me a second (f)
תִּסְגְּרוּ אֶת הַחַלּוֹן || close the window (pl)
:::

::: rule
Future for a command in speech, imperative on a page. *תִּכְתּוֹב* is what you
say to a colleague. *כְּתוֹב* is what an officer says and what a form says.
:::

This is not a matter of politeness. The future form is neither more nor less
polite than the imperative; it is simply the normal one. The true imperative
in a conversation sounds either military or faintly biblical, which is why it
survives best in fixed phrases: *סְלַח לִי* /slach li/ excuse me, *שֵׁב*, *בּוֹא
נֵלֵךְ* let's go.

::: warning
You will still read the imperative constantly. Instructions, recipes, signs,
exam papers and app buttons all use it: *הַזֵּן סִיסְמָה* enter a password,
*בְּחַר קוֹבֶץ* choose a file. Recognise it; produce the future.
:::

## The impersonal instruction

A public notice addresses nobody in particular, so it uses the infinitive,
usually softened by *נָא* /na/, please.

::: examples
נָא לֹא לְעַשֵּׁן || no smoking
נָא לִשְׁמוֹר עַל הַנִּיקָּיוֹן || please keep the place clean
נָא לֹא לְהַפְרִיעַ || do not disturb
לְהַשְׁאִיר אֶת הַדֶּלֶת סְגוּרָה || leave the door closed
:::

## Saying don't

::: rule
Negative commands use *אַל* plus the future. Never *לֹא*.
:::

::: examples
אַל תִּכְתּוֹב || don't write (m)
אַל תִּדְאֲגִי || don't worry (f)
אַל תִּשְׁכְּחוּ || don't forget (pl)
אַל תָּבוֹא מָחָר || don't come tomorrow
:::

*לֹא תִּכְתּוֹב* is not a command at all. It is a prediction: you will not
write. The one place *לֹא* does negate an instruction is the impersonal sign,
where it sits in front of an infinitive: *לֹא לְעַשֵּׁן*, *לֹא לְהַפְרִיעַ*.
That is the pattern above, and it is not a command to any particular person.

## Softening

Two moves cover almost everything. Add *בְּבַקָּשָׁה*, or turn the command into
a question.

::: examples
תִּכְתּוֹב לִי בְּבַקָּשָׁה || write to me please
אֶפְשָׁר לְקַבֵּל אֶת הַחֶשְׁבּוֹן? || could I have the bill?
אַתָּה יָכוֹל לַעֲזוֹר לִי? || can you help me?
אֶפְשָׁר לִשְׁאוֹל מַשֶּׁהוּ? || may I ask something?
תּוּכַל לְדַבֵּר לְאַט יוֹתֵר? || could you speak more slowly?
:::

*אֶפְשָׁר* /efshar/ plus an infinitive is the workhorse: it has no subject, so
nobody is being told to do anything, and it is the politest thing in daily use
that still gets you served.

::: note
Hebrew has no word doing the work of English "would you mind". Politeness is
carried by *בְּבַקָּשָׁה*, by the question form, and above all by tone. A flat
*תֵּן לִי קָפֶה* is not rude in a café; it is how the order is placed.
:::

::: exercise Tell someone what to do
Tell a man to write, in speech || *תִּכְתּוֹב* || the future is the spoken command
Tell a woman to write, on a form || *כִּתְבִי* || the written imperative, prefix removed
Tell a group to sit || *שְׁבוּ* || or *תֵּשְׁבוּ* in speech
Tell a man not to come || *אַל תָּבוֹא* || *אַל* plus the future, never *לֹא*
Put "no smoking" on a sign || *נָא לֹא לְעַשֵּׁן* || impersonal: *לֹא* plus the infinitive
Ask politely for the bill || *אֶפְשָׁר לְקַבֵּל אֶת הַחֶשְׁבּוֹן, בְּבַקָּשָׁה?* || a question with no subject in it
Make *תְּדַבְּרִי* an imperative || *דַּבְּרִי* || drop the תְּ prefix
Why is *לֹא תִּשְׁכַּח* wrong for "don't forget"? || it means "you will not forget" || a negative command needs *אַל*
:::
