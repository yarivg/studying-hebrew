# Ordinals, dates and the calendar

Counting things needed two sets of numbers, one per gender. Putting them in
order needs a third set, and this one is short: Hebrew builds proper ordinals
only as far as ten, and then gives up and reuses the cardinals.

## First to tenth

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | *רִאשׁוֹן* /rishon/ | *רִאשׁוֹנָה* |
| 2nd | *שֵׁנִי* /sheni/ | *שְׁנִיָּיה* |
| 3rd | *שְׁלִישִׁי* | *שְׁלִישִׁית* |
| 4th | *רְבִיעִי* | *רְבִיעִית* |
| 5th | *חֲמִישִׁי* | *חֲמִישִׁית* |
| 6th | *שִׁישִּׁי* | *שִׁישִּׁית* |
| 7th | *שְׁבִיעִי* | *שְׁבִיעִית* |
| 8th | *שְׁמִינִי* | *שְׁמִינִית* |
| 9th | *תְּשִׁיעִי* | *תְּשִׁיעִית* |
| 10th | *עֲשִׂירִי* | *עֲשִׂירִית* |

Eight of the ten are transparently built on the cardinal number with an *ִי*
ending. *רִאשׁוֹן* is the odd one out: it comes from *רֹאשׁ*, head, not from
*אֶחָד*, which is why it looks like nothing else in the column.

::: rule
Ordinals are ordinary adjectives. They follow the noun, agree with it in
gender and number, and take the article when the noun does.
:::

::: examples
הַפֶּרֶק הָרִאשׁוֹן || the first chapter
הַקּוֹמָה הַשְּׁנִיָּיה || the second floor
הַפַּעַם הַשְּׁלִישִׁית || the third time
שִׁיעוּר חֲמִישִׁי || a fifth lesson
:::

Above ten, Hebrew simply uses the cardinal number in the same slot.

::: examples
הַמֵּאָה הָעֶשְׂרִים || the twentieth century
פֶּרֶק שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה || chapter twelve
קוֹמָה שְׁלוֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה || the thirteenth floor
:::

::: note
*עֲשִׂירִית* is also "a tenth" as a fraction, and *שְׁלִישׁ* and *רֶבַע* are
"a third" and "a quarter". *רֶבַע* is the one you will use daily, telling the
time.
:::

## The days of the week

The days are already ordinals, and the week starts on Sunday.

| Hebrew | Day |
|---|---|
| *יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן* | Sunday |
| *יוֹם שֵׁנִי* | Monday |
| *יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי* | Tuesday |
| *יוֹם רְבִיעִי* | Wednesday |
| *יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי* | Thursday |
| *יוֹם שִׁישִּׁי* | Friday |
| *שַׁבָּת* | Saturday |

They are masculine because *יוֹם* is. *שַׁבָּת* has a name rather than a
number, and is the only one. In writing the days are abbreviated with a
letter: *יוֹם א׳*, *יוֹם ב׳*, up to *יוֹם ו׳*.

::: warning
*יוֹם שֵׁנִי* is Monday, not "the second day of your trip". An English speaker
counting off days will produce the wrong word about half the time in the first
month.
:::

## Writing and saying a date

Israel writes the day first: **22.9.2026**, or 22/9/2026, or *ה-22
בְּסֶפְּטֶמְבֶּר*. The American order never appears, which means a date written
3.5 is the third of May and nothing else.

Said aloud, a date is the masculine cardinal number with the article, then
*בְּ* and the month.

::: examples
הַשְּׁלוֹשָׁה בְּמַאי || the third of May
הָאֶחָד בְּיָנוּאָר || the first of January
הָעֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁנַיִם בְּסֶפְּטֶמְבֶּר || the twenty second of September
:::

The Gregorian months keep their European names, lightly respelled:

| | |
|---|---|
| *יָנוּאָר* | *יוּלִי* |
| *פֶבְּרוּאָר* | *אוֹגוּסְט* |
| *מֶרְץ* | *סֶפְּטֶמְבֶּר* |
| *אַפְּרִיל* | *אוֹקְטוֹבֶּר* |
| *מַאי* | *נוֹבֶמְבֶּר* |
| *יוּנִי* | *דֶּצֶמְבֶּר* |

## The Hebrew calendar

Twelve lunar months, with a thirteenth added in leap years, which is why the
holidays wander across the Gregorian year.

| | |
|---|---|
| *תִּשְׁרֵי* Tishrei, the year opens here | *נִיסָן* Nisan, around March and April |
| *חֶשְׁווָן* Cheshvan | *אִייָר* Iyar |
| *כִּסְלֵו* Kislev | *סִיוָון* Sivan |
| *טֵבֵת* Tevet | *תַּמּוּז* Tammuz |
| *שְׁבָט* Shvat | *אָב* Av |
| *אֲדָר* Adar | *אֱלוּל* Elul |

The months that matter are the ones with a holiday hanging off them:

| Month | Holiday |
|---|---|
| *תִּשְׁרֵי* | *רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה*, *יוֹם כִּיפּוּר*, *סוּכּוֹת* |
| *כִּסְלֵו* | *חֲנוּכָּה* |
| *אֲדָר* | *פּוּרִים* |
| *נִיסָן* | *פֶּסַח* |
| *סִיוָון* | *שָׁבוּעוֹת* |

::: rule
An Israeli runs their life on the Gregorian calendar. The Hebrew calendar is
for holidays, for religious documents and for gravestones. You need to
recognise the month names; you do not need to convert between the two.
:::

::: note
Hebrew years are written with letters rather than digits, so 5786 appears as
*תשפ"ו*. You will see it on a cornerstone and at the top of a synagogue
notice, and almost nowhere else.
:::

::: exercise Put it in order
Say "the first chapter" || *הַפֶּרֶק הָרִאשׁוֹן* || the ordinal follows the noun and takes the article
Say "the second floor" || *הַקּוֹמָה הַשְּׁנִיָּיה* || *קוֹמָה* is feminine
How do you say "the twelfth chapter"? || *פֶּרֶק שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה* || above ten the cardinal does the job
Which day is *יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי*? || Tuesday || the week starts on Sunday
What date is 3.5.2026? || the third of May || Israel writes the day first
Say "the third of May" || *הַשְּׁלוֹשָׁה בְּמַאי* || masculine cardinal with the article, then *בְּ*
Which month holds *פֶּסַח*? || *נִיסָן* || and *חֲנוּכָּה* falls in *כִּסְלֵו*
Why is *רִאשׁוֹן* not built on *אֶחָד*? || it comes from *רֹאשׁ*, head || the only ordinal that does not follow the pattern
:::
