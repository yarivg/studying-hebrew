# Roots and patterns

Hebrew words are not built by gluing pieces onto a stem the way English builds
*write, writer, writing*. They are built by pouring a set of consonants into a
mould. The consonants are the *שׁוֹרֶשׁ* /shoresh/, the root, and they carry
the meaning. The mould is the pattern, and it carries the grammar: what part
of speech this is, and what it does.

::: rule
The root says what the word is about. The pattern says what kind of word it
is. Neither means anything without the other.
:::

For verbs the mould is a *בִּנְיָין* /binyan/, and you already know four of
them. For nouns it is a *מִשְׁקָל* /mishkal/, literally a weight, and there are
a few dozen of those.

## One root all the way through: כ-ת-ב

| Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| כּוֹתֵב | pa'al present | writing |
| כָּתַב | pa'al past | he wrote |
| יִכְתּוֹב | pa'al future | he will write |
| לִכְתּוֹב | infinitive | to write |
| מִכְתָּב /mikhtav/ | noun | a letter |
| כְּתוֹבֶת /ktovet/ | noun | an address |
| כְּתִיבָה /ktiva/ | noun | writing, the act |
| כַּתָּב /katav/ | noun | a reporter |
| מַכְתֵּבָה /makhteva/ | noun | a writing desk |

Nine words, three letters. Unpoint them and every one of them still shows
כ, ת, ב in that order: כותב, כתב, יכתוב, לכתוב, מכתב, כתובת, כתיבה, כתב,
מכתבה. This is what Israelis see when they read unpointed text, and it is why
they can read it at all.

## A second root: ל-מ-ד

| Word | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| לוֹמֵד | pa'al present | learning, studying |
| לִימֵּד /limed/ | pi'el past | he taught |
| תַּלְמִיד /talmid/ | noun | a pupil |
| לִימּוּדִים /limudim/ | noun, plural | studies |
| מַלְמֵד /malmed/ | noun | an ox goad |

The first four are worth having. *מַלְמֵד* is included to be honest about the
shape rather than because you will need it: it is the instrument mishkal
applied to this root, it means the stick used to drive cattle, and it is
effectively biblical.

::: note
*מוֹרֶה*, a teacher, is not in this table, because it is not from ל-מ-ד. Its
root is י-ר-ה. The obvious-looking word is often from somewhere else, which
is exactly why you check the three letters rather than the general vibe.
:::

## The noun mishkalim worth recognising

Four patterns cover a large slice of the concrete vocabulary in the language.

| Mishkal | What it makes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| מַ.ְ.ֵ. | an instrument | מַפְתֵּחַ a key, מַזְלֵג a fork, מַחְשֵׁב a computer |
| מִ.ְ.ָ. | a place | מִשְׂרָד an office, מִטְבָּח a kitchen, מִדְבָּר a desert |
| .ַ.ָּ. | a profession | סַפָּר a barber, נַגָּר a carpenter, כַּתָּב a reporter |
| .ְ.ִי.ָה | the act of doing | כְּתִיבָה writing, קְרִיאָה reading, שְׁתִייָּה drinking |

::: tip
A word that starts with *מַ* and has three more consonants is very often a
tool. A word that starts with *מִ* is very often a place. It is a guess, not a
law, and it is right often enough to be worth making.
:::

::: examples
מַחְשֵׁב || a computer: the instrument for ח-שׁ-ב, to think, to calculate
מִדְבָּר || a desert: the place of ד-ב-ר
נַגָּר || a carpenter: the profession of נ-ג-ר
שְׁתִייָּה || drinking: the act of שׁ-ת-ה
:::

## Why the dictionary is organised this way

A Hebrew dictionary sorts by root, not alphabetically by word. *הִתְלַבֵּשׁ*
is not filed under ה, it is under ל-ב-שׁ, next to *לְבוּשׁ* clothing and
*מַלְבּוּשׁ* a garment. The first time you look one up it is annoying. After
that it is the point: you meet one word and collect three.

::: warning
The meaning link is real often enough to rely on and stretched often enough to
embarrass you. Take ס-פ-ר: *סֵפֶר* a book, *סִפְרִייָּה* a library,
*סִיפֵּר* he told a story, *מִסְפָּר* a number, and *סַפָּר* a barber. Books,
counting and haircuts are one root. Use the root to remember a word you have
already met. Do not use it to guess a meaning you have not.
:::

::: exercise Root and pattern
What is the root of *מִכְתָּב*? || כ-ת-ב || the מ belongs to the mishkal, not the root
What is the root of *הִתְלַבֵּשׁ*? || ל-ב-שׁ || *הִתְ* is the binyan
*מַזְלֵג* is built on which mishkal, and what does that tell you? || *מַ.ְ.ֵ.*, the instrument pattern, so it is a tool || the same pattern as *מַפְתֵּחַ* and *מַחְשֵׁב*
What kind of word is *מִטְבָּח*, from its shape alone? || a place || *מִ.ְ.ָ.* is the place mishkal
Form the act-noun of ק-ר-א || *קְרִיאָה* || the *.ְ.ִי.ָה* pattern
What does a *כַּתָּב* do, and how can you tell? || writes, as a reporter: the root is כ-ת-ב and the shape is the profession mishkal || same pattern as *סַפָּר* and *נַגָּר*
Is *מוֹרֶה* from ל-מ-ד? || no, from י-ר-ה || a plausible root is not the same as the right one
Why is a Hebrew dictionary sorted by root? || because the root is the constant, and every form of every related word contains it || so one lookup gives you a family
:::
