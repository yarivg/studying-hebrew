# Preposition tables

Hebrew does not put a pronoun after a preposition. It puts the pronoun on the
preposition, and the result is one word. Every declining preposition is here,
each with all ten persons.

## Two sets of endings

There are two declensions and only two, so the whole system is two lists of
ten endings.

| Person | Singular set | Plural set |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | *ִי* | *ַיי* |
| אַתָּה | *ְךָ* | *ֶיךָ* |
| אַתְּ | *ֵךְ* | *ַיִיךְ* |
| הוּא | *וֹ* | *ָיו* |
| הִיא | *ָהּ* | *ֶיהָ* |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | *ֵנוּ* | *ֵינוּ* |
| אַתֶּם | *ְכֶם* | *ֵיכֶם* |
| אַתֶּן | *ְכֶן* | *ֵיכֶן* |
| הֵם | *ָם* | *ֵיהֶם* |
| הֵן | *ָן* | *ֵיהֶן* |

::: rule
The singular set is the one a singular noun takes, as in סִפְרִי, my book. The
plural set is the one a plural noun takes, as in סְפָרַיי, my books. A
preposition in the plural set is not plural in meaning. It only wears plural
clothes.
:::

::: tip
To tell which set a preposition belongs to, look at the הוּא form. If it ends
in *וֹ* it is the singular set: לוֹ, בּוֹ, אֶצְלוֹ. If it ends in *ָיו* it is
the plural set: עָלָיו, אֵלָיו, לְפָנָיו. That one form decides the other nine.
:::

A preposition ending in *ֵי* on its own, לִפְנֵי and אַחֲרֵי, is already
wearing the plural ending before you add anything, and it takes the plural
set. עַל and אֶל look singular and take the plural set anyway, because both
were once עֲלֵי and אֱלֵי.

### אֶת: the object marker

The endings go on a stem אוֹת־ that exists for no other purpose.

| Person | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | אוֹתִי | me |
| אַתָּה | אוֹתְךָ | you (m) |
| אַתְּ | אוֹתָךְ | you (f) |
| הוּא | אוֹתוֹ | him, it |
| הִיא | אוֹתָהּ | her, it |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | אוֹתָנוּ | us |
| אַתֶּם | אֶתְכֶם | you (m pl) |
| אַתֶּן | אֶתְכֶן | you (f pl) |
| הֵם | אוֹתָם | them (m) |
| הֵן | אוֹתָן | them (f) |

::: warning
Two rows break the stem: אֶתְכֶם and אֶתְכֶן, not אוֹתְכֶם. The אוֹתְכֶם form
is common in speech and is still corrected in writing. Everything else in the
column takes אוֹת־.
:::

### מִן: from

The odd one out. Four of its forms double the נ back into view.

| Person | Form | |
|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי | מִמֶּנִּי | from me |
| אַתָּה | מִמְּךָ | from you (m) |
| אַתְּ | מִמֵּךְ | from you (f) |
| הוּא | מִמֶּנּוּ | from him |
| הִיא | מִמֶּנָּה | from her |
| אֲנַחְנוּ | מֵאִיתָּנוּ | from us |
| אַתֶּם | מִכֶּם | from you (m pl) |
| אַתֶּן | מִכֶּן | from you (f pl) |
| הֵם | מֵהֶם | from them (m) |
| הֵן | מֵהֶן | from them (f) |

::: warning
מִמֶּנּוּ is "from him" and, in the Academy's grammar, also "from us". Speech
refuses the ambiguity and says מֵאִיתָּנוּ for "from us". Use מֵאִיתָּנוּ.
:::

:::

## Prepositions that do not decline

These take a pronoun as a separate word, or push the ending onto something
else. Trying to decline them is the mistake that gives a learner away.

| Preposition | | What you say instead |
|---|---|---|
| עַד | until, as far as | עַד אֵלַיי, עַד עַכְשָׁיו |
| בְּלִי | without | בִּלְעָדַיי: a different word takes the endings |
| חוּץ מִ | apart from | חוּץ מִמֶּנִּי: the מִן inside declines |
| לְפִי | according to | לְפִי מַה שֶּׁאָמַרְתָּ |
| בִּמְקוֹם | instead of | בִּמְקוֹמִי: the noun מָקוֹם takes the ending |
| עַל יְדֵי | by, by means of | עַל יְדֵי הַמּוֹרֶה, with a noun after it |

::: note
לְיַד, next to, sits on the line. לְיָדִי and לְיָדוֹ are normal in speech, and
so is לְיַד הַבַּיִת with a noun. Both are safe.
:::
