# How to use this course

## The four parts

Part I is the foundation: the letters, the points, and enough grammar to build
a sentence. Twenty-six chapters, and nothing in it can be skipped, because
everything after it assumes all of it. Give it seven or eight weeks. Part II
is the past and the future and the system behind them, twenty chapters, and it
is where the language stops feeling like a list of words. Part III is
refinement: the passive, longer sentences, register, idiom. Thirteen chapters,
and you can take them in any order once you are in it. Part IV is this one,
tables to look things up in, not to read.

End to end that is roughly six months at three or four sessions a week. Faster
is possible and usually costs you Part I, which is the part you cannot afford
to owe yourself later.

## A route through Part I

| Week | Chapters | What you are doing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 to 4 | the letters and the points, with the audio on for every example |
| 2 | 5 to 8 | ו and י as vowels, stress, reading unpointed, the first exchange |
| 3 | 9 to 12 | nouns, the article, pronouns, adjectives |
| 4 | 13 to 16 | this and that, questions, numbers, יֵשׁ and אֵין |
| 5 | 17 to 19 | prepositions with endings, אֶת, the present tense |
| 6 | 20 to 22 | weak roots in the present, the other binyanim, negation |
| 7 | 23 to 26 | infinitives, time and place, סְמִיכוּת, הָיָה and יִהְיֶה |

Week 1 is the only week that is pure memorisation, and it is the only one
where the audio is not optional. You are not learning what the letters look
like; you are learning to hear a sound and reach for a shape. That takes
repetition and it takes sound.

From Part II onwards, a chapter every two or three days is the right pace. The
chapters are longer, and each one is doing something the one before it set up.
If a week goes by and you have done one chapter, that is fine. If a week goes
by and you have done five, you have read five chapters and learned none.

## Working a chapter

Read the chapter through once without stopping. Then go back and do the inline
exercises, out loud, writing the answer before you look at it. Reading an
answer you have not attempted teaches nothing; it only feels like it does.

Then take the chapter test. The score is evidence, not a verdict. When you
have the result, mark yourself at the end of the chapter honestly, and honest
means asking whether you could produce this tomorrow without the page in front
of you, not whether the questions felt fair. A chapter you passed at 80 per
cent and could not use in a sentence is not learned.

The flashcards schedule themselves. Do the cards that come up, every day if
you can, and do not add cards manually to get ahead: the deck is built from
the chapters you have marked, and pulling words forward puts vocabulary in
front of you before the grammar that holds it. Read one passage a week from
the reading section, whichever one is closest to your level, and do not look
up more than five words in it.

## The nikud toggle

The switch in the top bar takes the points away. Use it deliberately, not for
comfort: read every chapter twice, once with the points and once without.

The first pass is for accuracy. The second pass is the skill you are actually
building, because no Hebrew you will ever meet outside this course carries
nikud. The second pass will be slow and it will feel like a step backwards.
That is what it is supposed to feel like. The words you can read unpointed are
the words you know.

## When a chapter will not stick

Go back one chapter. Do not reread the same one.

Rereading a chapter that is not landing is the most common way to waste a
week, because the problem is almost never in the chapter you are on. The
adjectives will not agree because the noun genders are shaky. The future tense
will not build because the root is not visible. Go back one chapter, redo its
exercises, and come forward again. If the chapter before is solid, go back two.
You will usually find the gap within three.

## What this course does not do

Handwriting. Hebrew cursive is a separate alphabet in practice, and you will
need it the day you fill in a form in Israel and not before. Learn it from a
chart in an afternoon when that day comes.

Conversation. You can reach B2 reading and writing on your own, and you cannot
reach it speaking. Everything here is designed so that when you do find a
person to talk to, you arrive with the grammar already in place and spend your
time on the only thing that needs another human: saying it fast enough.
