# Alef-bet and nikud chart

Everything from chapters 1 to 5 of Part I on one page: the letters, the two
dots that are not vowels, the vowel signs, and the two letters that do a
vowel's job. Nothing here is explained, because it is all explained there.
This is the page to keep open while you read.

## The twenty-two letters

::: note
Read each row from the right. The final form is the shape the letter takes as
the last letter of a word; it is the same letter, not a new one.
:::

| Letter | Final | Name | Name said | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| א | | אָלֶף | /alef/ | silent | אוֹר /or/ light |
| ב | | בֵּית | /bet/ | b with a dagesh, v without | בַּיִת /bayit/ house |
| ג | | גִּימֶל | /gimel/ | g | גָּדוֹל /gadol/ big |
| ד | | דָּלֶת | /dalet/ | d | דֶּלֶת /delet/ door |
| ה | | הֵא | /hei/ | h, often silent at the end | הַר /har/ mountain |
| ו | | וָו | /vav/ | v, or a vowel | וֶרֶד /vered/ rose |
| ז | | זַיִן | /zayin/ | z | זְמַן /zman/ time |
| ח | | חֵית | /khet/ | kh, in the throat | חַם /kham/ hot |
| ט | | טֵית | /tet/ | t | טוֹב /tov/ good |
| י | | יוֹד | /yod/ | y, or a vowel | יָד /yad/ hand |
| כ | ך | כַּף | /kaf/ | k with a dagesh, kh without | כֶּסֶף /kesef/ money |
| ל | | לָמֶד | /lamed/ | l | לֵב /lev/ heart |
| מ | ם | מֵם | /mem/ | m | מַיִם /mayim/ water |
| נ | ן | נוּן | /nun/ | n | נָכוֹן /nakhon/ correct |
| ס | | סָמֶךְ | /samekh/ | s | סֵפֶר /sefer/ book |
| ע | | עַיִן | /ayin/ | silent | עִיר /ir/ city |
| פ | ף | פֵּא | /pei/ | p with a dagesh, f without | פֶּה /pe/ mouth |
| צ | ץ | צָדִי | /tzadi/ | tz | צָהוֹב /tzahov/ yellow |
| ק | | קוֹף | /kuf/ | k | קָטָן /katan/ small |
| ר | | רֵישׁ | /resh/ | r, in the throat | רֹאשׁ /rosh/ head |
| ש | | שִׁין | /shin/ | sh, or s with the dot on the left | שָׁלוֹם /shalom/ hello |

::: warning
א and ע are both silent, ק and כּ are both k, ט and תּ are both t, and ח and
כ are both kh. Four pairs you cannot tell apart by ear. You learn which one a
word takes when you learn the word.
:::

## בְּגַ"ד כְּפַ"ת: the six letters that take a dagesh

| Without dagesh | With dagesh | Difference today |
|---|---|---|
| ב /v/ | בּ /b/ | audible |
| ג /g/ | גּ /g/ | none |
| ד /d/ | דּ /d/ | none |
| כ /kh/ | כּ /k/ | audible |
| פ /f/ | פּ /p/ | audible |
| ת /t/ | תּ /t/ | none |

::: rule
Only three of the six still change: בּ/ב, כּ/כ, פּ/פ. The dot on ג, ד and ת is
written because the pointing is written properly, and it is ignored when you
read aloud.
:::

## Shin and sin

| Letter | Name | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| שׁ | שִׁין /shin/ | sh | שָׁלוֹם /shalom/ hello |
| שׂ | שִׂין /sin/ | s | שָׂמֵחַ /sameakh/ happy |

The dot is on the right for shin, on the left for sin. This one is not a
dagesh: it decides which letter you are looking at, not how hard it is said.

## The nikud signs

::: note
Each sign is drawn here under a bare א so that it is visible on its own. In a
real word it sits under, inside or above the consonant it follows, and it is
always read after that consonant: בַּ is "ba", never "ab".
:::

| Sign | Name | Name said | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| אָ | קָמָץ | /kamatz/ | a | יָד /yad/ hand |
| אַ | פַּתָּח | /patakh/ | a | גַּן /gan/ garden |
| אֵ | צֵירֵי | /tzere/ | e | כֵּן /ken/ yes |
| אֶ | סֶגּוֹל | /segol/ | e | דֶּלֶת /delet/ door |
| אִ | חִירִיק | /khirik/ | i | אִישׁ /ish/ man |
| אֹ | חוֹלָם חָסֵר | /kholam khaser/ | o | רֹאשׁ /rosh/ head |
| אוֹ | חוֹלָם מָלֵא | /kholam male/ | o | שָׁלוֹם /shalom/ hello |
| אֻ | קוּבּוּץ | /kubutz/ | u | שֻׁלְחָן, written שׁוּלְחָן here |
| אוּ | שׁוּרוּק | /shuruk/ | u | שׁוּק /shuk/ market |
| אְ | שְׁוָוא | /shva/ | none, or a very short e | סְפָרִים /sfarim/ books |
| אֲ | חֲטָף פַּתָּח | /khataf patakh/ | a very short a | אֲנִי /ani/ I |
| אֱ | חֲטָף סֶגּוֹל | /khataf segol/ | a very short e | אֱמֶת /emet/ truth |
| אֳ | חֲטָף קָמָץ | /khataf kamatz/ | a very short o | צָהֳרַיִים /tzohorayim/ noon |

::: rule
Five sounds, thirteen signs. קָמָץ and פַּתָּח are both a, צֵירֵי and סֶגּוֹל
are both e, the three חוֹלָם spellings are all o, קוּבּוּץ and שׁוּרוּק are both
u. You read them apart by shape and you cannot hear them apart at all.
:::

::: warning
קָמָץ קָטָן /kamatz katan/ is a kamatz read as o, not a. It is the same sign.
The set of words it appears in is small and learnable: כָּל /kol/ all,
צָהֳרַיִים, חָכְמָה /khokhma/ wisdom. Meet each one as it comes.
:::

::: tip
The three chataf signs belong to the four gutturals א ה ח ע, which refuse a
plain shva. If you see a shva with a vowel beside it, look at the letter: it
will be one of those four almost every time.
:::

## ו and י doing a vowel's job

This course writes full spelling, so ו and י are on the page as well as the
points. Take the vowel reading first: it is right more often than not.

| Form | Job | Example | Unpointed |
|---|---|---|---|
| ו | the consonant v | וֶרֶד /vered/ rose, וְ /ve/ and | ורד |
| וֹ | the vowel o | שָׁלוֹם | שלום |
| וּ | the vowel u | שׁוּק | שוק |

A doubled וו is the consonant v inside a word: מִצְוָוה /mitzva/ a
commandment, written מצווה.

| Form | Job | Example | Unpointed |
|---|---|---|---|
| י | the consonant y | יֶלֶד /yeled/ child | ילד |
| י | the vowel i, and e from a צֵירֵי | עִיר, בֵּית /bet/ house of | עיר, בית |

A doubled יי is the consonant y between vowels: בִּנְיָינִים /binyanim/,
written בניינים.
