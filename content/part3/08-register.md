# Register: formal, spoken, slang

Hebrew's levels sit unusually far apart. A written tradition of two thousand
years and a spoken life of not much more than a hundred years produced a
formal register lifted more or less intact out of books, and a street register
that took its furniture from Arabic, Yiddish and English. The gap between them
is wider than the gap between written and spoken English, and an Israeli
crosses it several times a day without thinking about it.

The point of this chapter is not to teach you a new grammar. Every form in it
you already have. The point is to hear which shelf a sentence came off, and to
choose your own shelf on purpose.

## One sentence, three heights

**Send me the letter you wrote to your brother.**

::: examples
שְׁלַח אֵלַיי אֶת הַמִּכְתָּב אֲשֶׁר כָּתַבְתָּ לְאָחִיךָ. || formal
תִּשְׁלַח לִי אֶת הַמִּכְתָּב שֶׁכָּתַבְתָּ לָאָח שֶׁלְּךָ. || spoken
יַאלְלָה, תִּזְרוֹק לִי מַה שֶׁכָּתַבְתָּ לָאָח שֶׁלְּךָ. || slang
:::

Four things moved. The true imperative *שְׁלַח* /shlakh/ became the future
*תִּשְׁלַח*, which is what an Israeli actually says for a command. *אֲשֶׁר*
/asher/, the full relative pronoun, shrank to *שֶׁ*. The possessive suffix
*לְאָחִיךָ* /le'akhikha/ unpacked into *לָאָח שֶׁלְּךָ*. And the slang line
replaced the verb entirely: *לִזְרוֹק* /lizrok/, to throw, is what you do to a
file you are sending.

**The decision was taken by the committee and published last night.**

::: examples
הַהַחְלָטָה הִתְקַבְּלָה בַּוַּועֲדָה וּפוּרְסְמָה אֶמֶשׁ. || formal
בַּוַּועֲדָה הֶחְלִיטוּ, וּפִרְסְמוּ אֶת זֶה אֶתְמוֹל בָּעֶרֶב. || spoken
הֶחְלִיטוּ שָׁם מַשֶּׁהוּ אֶתְמוֹל, לֵךְ תֵּדַע מָה. || slang
:::

*פּוּרְסְמָה* is *פּוּעַל* /pu'al/, and it is doing exactly what the passive
binyanim do best: naming the event and leaving the agent out. Speech will not
use it. Speech says *פִּרְסְמוּ*, third person plural with nobody in
particular as the subject, which is the spoken language's entire passive.

**Don't tell him what I said, he'll get angry.**

::: examples
אַל תְּסַפֵּר לוֹ אֶת אֲשֶׁר אָמַרְתִּי, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁיִּכְעַס. || formal
אַל תְּסַפֵּר לוֹ מַה שֶׁאָמַרְתִּי, כִּי הוּא יִכְעַס. || spoken
אַל תַּגִּיד לוֹ כְּלוּם, הוּא יִתְחַרְפֵּן. || slang
:::

Note that *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁיִּכְעַס* drops the pronoun, because the prefix *יִ*
already carries "he". Spoken Hebrew puts *הוּא* back in. Formal Hebrew trusts
the morphology; spoken Hebrew repeats itself, the way speech everywhere does.

## The markers, side by side

| | Formal | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| that, which | *אֲשֶׁר* | *שֶׁ* |
| my book | *סִפְרִי* | *הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי* |
| because | *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ*, *כֵּיוָון שֶׁ* | *בִּגְלַל שֶׁ*, *כִּי* |
| write! | *כְּתוֹב!* | *תִּכְתּוֹב!* |
| passive | *פּוּעַל*, *הוּפְעַל* | third person plural |
| by (agent) | *בִּידֵי*, *עַל יְדֵי* | left out |
| when | *כַּאֲשֶׁר* | *כְּשֶׁ* |
| now | *כָּעֵת* | *עַכְשָׁיו* |
| last night | *אֶמֶשׁ* | *אֶתְמוֹל בָּעֶרֶב* |
| but | *אוּלָם*, *אַךְ* | *אֲבָל* |
| also | *אַף*, *כְּמוֹ כֵן* | *גַּם* |
| very | *בְּיוֹתֵר* | *מְאוֹד*, *נוֹרָא* |

::: rule
The formal register is not "better Hebrew". It is a dialect used for laws,
academic prose, poetry, ceremonies and news copy. In a conversation it is as
out of place as legal English at a bus stop.
:::

## Where the slang came from

Israeli slang is a sediment, and the layers are datable.

**Arabic**, mostly Palestinian and Levantine, arrived through contact and
through the army, and it is the biggest layer by far. It supplies the words
for mood, approval and refusal, which is exactly the vocabulary a language
borrows.

**Yiddish** supplies the words for people and for chaos, usually with an
affectionate sting. *בַּלָגָן* /balagan/ is the famous one, and it is not
Yiddish: it is Persian, through Turkish, into Russian, and into Hebrew with
the Russian-speaking immigrants, where it meant a fairground booth before it
meant a mess.

**English** supplies everything technical and half of everything young. Hebrew
takes an English word, pulls three or four consonants out of it and runs them
through a binyan: *לְגַגֵּל* /legagel/, to google, *לְסַנְכְרֵן*, to sync,
*לְטַרְגֵּט*, to target. The binyan machinery from Part II is doing the work,
on roots that did not exist twenty years ago.

**The army** supplies a tone as much as a vocabulary: *לְהִתְחַפֵּף*
/lehitkhafef/, to clear off, *לְהִשְׁתַּפְשֵׁף*, to be worn in by hard duty,
and a wall of abbreviations. Every Israeli did the same three
years and came out with the same hundred words, which is why army slang is
national rather than sectional.

| Slang | Literally | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| יַאלְלָה | Arabic, "O God" | come on, let's go, right then |
| סַבָּבָּה | Arabic, "fine, excellent" | great, fine, no problem |
| אַחְלָה | Arabic, "the sweetest" | top, excellent (before a noun too) |
| וָואלָּה | Arabic, "by God" | really? / honestly, yes |
| מַבְּסוּט | Arabic, "pleased" | happy with how things went |
| דּוּגְרִי | Turkish via Arabic, "straight" | straight talk, no diplomacy |
| חָלָאס | Arabic, "finished" | enough, stop it |
| פַדִיחָה | Arabic, "a scandal" | an embarrassment, a cringe moment |
| בַּלָגָן | Persian via Russian, "a booth" | a mess, chaos |
| נוּדְנִיק | Yiddish and Slavic, "a borer" | a pest who will not let a thing go |
| כַּפָּרָה | Hebrew, "an atonement" | darling, sweetheart (also mock-pleading) |
| פִּיצְקֶלֶה | Yiddish, "a tiny thing" | a little one, a tiny amount |

::: examples
יַאלְלָה, בּוֹא נֵלֵךְ, אֲנַחְנוּ מְאַחֲרִים. || come on, let's go, we're late
הַכּוֹל סַבָּבָּה, אַל תִּדְאַג. || it's all fine, don't worry
תַּגִּיד לִי דּוּגְרִי מָה אַתָּה חוֹשֵׁב. || tell me straight what you think
הָיָה בַּלָגָן שָׁלֵם בַּמִּשְׂרָד. || the office was complete chaos
חָלָאס עִם הַסִּיפּוּר הַזֶּה. || enough with this story
:::

::: warning
*שְׁלִימָזֵל* /shlimazel/, from Yiddish *shlim* (bad) plus Hebrew *מַזָּל*
/mazal/, is a hopeless unlucky case. It is old-fashioned now, and a foreigner
producing it sounds like they learned Hebrew from a 1950s film. The same goes
for most of the Yiddish layer apart from *בַּלָגָן* and *נוּדְנִיק*.
:::

## What to aim at

Aim at the spoken standard: *שֶׁ*, *שֶׁל*, *בִּגְלַל שֶׁ*, the future as an
imperative. That is the register of every conversation you will have, and it
is also perfectly acceptable in an email. Read the formal register; do not
produce it. Saying *אֲשֶׁר* out loud, or *הֲרֵינִי מוֹדֶה לְךָ* instead of
*תּוֹדָה*, does not sound educated. It sounds like a machine, or like someone
quoting.

::: tip
The safest slang for a learner is the approval vocabulary: *סַבָּבָּה*,
*אַחְלָה*, *יַאלְלָה*, *בְּסֵדֶר גָּמוּר*. Nobody minds an accent on those.
The unsafe slang is anything rude, anything about someone's mother, and army
terms you did not earn. Wrong slang in a foreign accent does not read as
casual, it reads as a misfire.
:::

::: note
Israelis mix levels freely inside one sentence, and that is not a mistake:
*הַוַּועֲדָה קִיבְּלָה הַחְלָטָה, וְזֶה עַל הַפָּנִים*, "the committee took a
decision, and it's rubbish". Bureaucratic noun phrase, street verdict. The
mixture is the actual register of the country.
:::

Make *הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי* formal || *סִפְרִי* || the possessive suffix, not *שֶׁל*
Make *הָאִישׁ שֶׁרָאִיתִי* formal || *הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר רָאִיתִי* || *אֲשֶׁר* is the written relative
Make *כְּתוֹב אֶת הַשֵּׁם* spoken || *תִּכְתּוֹב אֶת הַשֵּׁם* || speech uses the future as an imperative
Make *הַמִּכְתָּב נִשְׁלַח* into spoken Hebrew || *שָׁלְחוּ אֶת הַמִּכְתָּב* || third person plural is the spoken passive
Which is formal, *בִּגְלַל שֶׁ* or *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ*? || *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ* || *בִּגְלַל שֶׁ* is spoken and very common
Where does *בַּלָגָן* come from? || Persian, through Turkish and Russian, not Yiddish || it meant a fairground booth
What does *דּוּגְרִי* mean? || straight, without diplomacy || *תַּגִּיד לִי דּוּגְרִי*
Name two slang words a learner can use safely || any two of *סַבָּבָּה*, *אַחְלָה*, *יַאלְלָה*, *וָואלָּה* || approval words travel; insults do not
:::
