# Idioms and set phrases

An idiom is a phrase whose meaning you cannot reach from its words, which
makes it the one part of a language that grammar cannot help you with. Hebrew
has a lot of them, and because Israeli speech is direct and short, they carry
more of the weight of a conversation than they would in English. A person who
knows four hundred words and thirty idioms sounds better than a person who
knows a thousand words and none.

Each table below has a column marked "use". **Safe** means you can say it
tomorrow and nobody will blink. **Careful** means it is real Hebrew but
depends on tone, age or relationship, and a foreign accent on top of it will
be noticed. **Avoid** means: understand it, do not say it. The reason is
almost never that the phrase is rude. It is that it is intimate, and intimacy
that has not been earned sounds like an impression.

## Agreeing and disagreeing

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| בְּסֵדֶר גָּמוּר | in complete order | absolutely fine, done | safe |
| אֵין בְּעָיָה | there is no problem | no problem | safe |
| בְּטַח | surely | of course | safe |
| אֵין מַצָּב | there is no situation | no chance, no way | safe |
| לֹא בָּא בְּחֶשְׁבּוֹן | it does not enter the account | out of the question | safe |
| מָה פִּתְאוֹם | what, suddenly? | don't be ridiculous, certainly not | careful |
| בְּחַיֶּיךָ | by your life | (1) seriously? (2) go on, do me a favour | careful |
| וָואלָּה | (Arabic) by God | huh, really / yes, honestly | careful |

::: examples
בְּסֵדֶר גָּמוּר, נִיפָּגֵשׁ בְּשֶׁבַע. || fine, we'll meet at seven
אֵין מַצָּב שֶׁאֲנִי קָם בְּחָמֵשׁ בַּבּוֹקֶר. || no chance I'm getting up at five
מָה פִּתְאוֹם? אֲנִי מְשַׁלֵּם. || don't be silly, I'm paying
:::

::: note
*בְּחַיֶּיךָ* changes its whole meaning with intonation. Flat and falling, it
is "you're kidding". Rising and drawn out, it is a request: *בְּחַיֶּיךָ,
תַּעֲזוֹר לִי*, "come on, help me out". Get the tone wrong and you have asked
a favour when you meant to express doubt.
:::

## Enthusiasm

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| סוֹף הַדֶּרֶךְ | the end of the road | the best there is | safe |
| מְטוֹרָף | insane | incredible | safe |
| פְּצָצָה | a bomb | fantastic, a knockout | safe |
| לֹא יְאוּמַן | it cannot be believed | unbelievable, either way | safe |
| חֲבָל עַל הַזְּמַן | a waste of time | outstanding | careful |
| הוֹרֵס | destroys | devastatingly good | careful |
| מַגְנִיב | makes you steal | cool | careful |

::: warning
*חֲבָל עַל הַזְּמַן* is the trap in this chapter. Its words say "don't waste
your time" and it normally means the exact opposite: *הַקּוֹנְצֶרְט הָיָה
חֲבָל עַל הַזְּמַן* is high praise. It can still be used literally, and only
tone and context separate the two. Understand it before you use it.
:::

::: examples
הַסֶּרֶט הָיָה חֲבָל עַל הַזְּמַן. || the film was amazing
הַחוּמוּס שָׁם סוֹף הַדֶּרֶךְ. || the hummus there is the best there is
עָשִׂיתָ עֲבוֹדָה מְטוֹרֶפֶת. || you did an insane job
:::

## Complaint, and getting attention

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| עַל הַפָּנִים | on the face | terrible, rubbish | safe |
| נִמְאַס לִי | it has become loathsome to me | I have had enough | safe |
| דַּי כְּבָר | enough already | stop it | safe |
| שִׂים לֵב | put a heart | note, pay attention | safe |
| מָה הַקֶּטַע | what's the segment? | what's going on with that? | careful |
| בְּאֵיזֶה עוֹלָם אַתָּה חַי | what world are you living in | you have no idea | careful |
| יָצָא לִי מֵהָאַף | it came out of my nose | it was ruined for me, I am sick of it | avoid |

::: examples
הָאוֹכֶל בַּמִּסְעָדָה הַזֹּאת עַל הַפָּנִים. || the food in that restaurant is terrible
נִמְאַס לִי לְחַכּוֹת, אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ. || I'm sick of waiting, I'm going
שִׂים לֵב שֶׁהַמִּשְׂרָד סָגוּר בְּיוֹם שִׁישִּׁי. || note that the office is closed on Friday
:::

## Time

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| עוֹד מְעַט | a little more | in a moment, shortly | safe |
| תֵּיכֶף וּמִיָּד | presently and at once | right this second | safe |
| אַחַת וּלְתָמִיד | once and for always | once and for all | safe |
| מִזְּמַן | from time | ages ago, long since | safe |
| בְּסוֹפוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר | at the end of a thing | ultimately, in the end | safe |
| לְעֵת עַתָּה | for the present time | for the time being | formal writing |
| בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת | between the suns | dusk; an in-between, undecided state | careful |

*בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת* is the gap between sunset and full dark, which matters
in religious law because the day changes inside it. In ordinary speech it gets
used for anything caught between two states.

::: examples
תֵּיכֶף וּמִיָּד אֲנִי מַגִּיעַ. || I'll be there in one second
הוּא עָזַב אֶת הָעִיר מִזְּמַן. || he left the city ages ago
בּוֹא נִסְגּוֹר אֶת זֶה אַחַת וּלְתָמִיד. || let's settle this once and for all
:::

## People

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| בֶּן אָדָם | son of man | a human being; also "mate" in address | safe |
| טִיפּוּס | a type | a character, a piece of work | safe |
| פִּיצוּחַ | a cracking open | a brilliant solve, a problem cracked | safe |
| נְשָׁמָה | a soul | love, sweetheart, to anyone at all | careful |
| אָח שֶׁלִּי | my brother | mate, bro | careful |
| גֶּבֶר גֶּבֶר | man man | a proper man, a legend, half joking | careful |
| כַּפָּרָה עָלֶיךָ | an atonement upon you | darling, you angel | avoid |

::: examples
תַּקְשִׁיב, בֶּן אָדָם, זֶה לֹא הוֹגֵן. || listen, mate, that isn't fair
תּוֹדָה, נְשָׁמָה. || thanks, love
הוּא טִיפּוּס, אֲבָל אֲנִי אוֹהֵב אוֹתוֹ. || he's a piece of work, but I like him
:::

## Religious in origin, secular in use

Israel is a country where thoroughly secular people say "with God's help"
several times a day and mean "I hope so". These phrases have been emptied of
belief and kept as grammar.

| Phrase | Literally | Means | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| בְּעֶזְרַת הַשֵּׁם | with the help of the Name | hopefully, God willing | safe |
| תּוֹדָה לָאֵל | thanks to God | thank goodness | safe |
| חַס וְחָלִילָה | mercy and forbid | God forbid, heaven forfend | safe |
| בְּשָׁעָה טוֹבָה | in a good hour | congratulations, and about time | safe |
| בָּרוּךְ הַשֵּׁם | blessed is the Name | fine, all good, as an answer | careful |
| אִם יִרְצֶה הַשֵּׁם | if the Name wills it | hopefully | careful |
| בְּלִי עַיִן הָרָע | without the evil eye | touch wood, knock on wood | careful |

::: examples
נִתְרָאֶה מָחָר, בְּעֶזְרַת הַשֵּׁם. || see you tomorrow, God willing
חַס וְחָלִילָה שֶׁיִּקְרֶה לוֹ מַשֶּׁהוּ. || God forbid anything happens to him
בְּשָׁעָה טוֹבָה, סוֹף סוֹף מָצָאתָ דִּירָה! || at last, you've found a flat!
:::

::: rule
The religious phrases are marked "careful" not because they are holy but
because they place you. *בָּרוּךְ הַשֵּׁם* as an answer to "how are you" reads
as traditional or religious. *בְּעֶזְרַת הַשֵּׁם* does not: everybody says it.
:::

::: tip
Learn idioms in whole sentences, never as a list. An idiom you have only met
as a table row comes out at the wrong speed and in the wrong place. An idiom
you have heard in three sentences comes out right.
:::

::: exercise Say it like an Israeli
Say "no chance" || *אֵין מַצָּב* || the everyday refusal
What does *חֲבָל עַל הַזְּמַן* usually mean? || outstanding, amazing || its words say the opposite, which is the trap
Say "the food is terrible" || *הָאוֹכֶל עַל הַפָּנִים* || literally "on the face"
What does *נִמְאַס לִי* mean? || I have had enough, I am fed up || *נִמְאַס לִי לְחַכּוֹת*
Say "ages ago" || *מִזְּמַן* || one word, very common
What is *בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת*? || dusk, and by extension any in-between state || between sunset and dark
Which is safer for a learner, *נְשָׁמָה* or *בֶּן אָדָם*? || *בֶּן אָדָם* || terms of endearment need a relationship behind them
Say "see you tomorrow, God willing" || *נִתְרָאֶה מָחָר, בְּעֶזְרַת הַשֵּׁם* || said by religious and secular speakers alike
:::
