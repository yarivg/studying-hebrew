# Clauses of time and condition

A time clause is easy to build in Hebrew and easy to get wrong, because the
difficulty is not vocabulary. It is the tense you put inside it, and the
English habit that has to be unlearned before the right tense will come out.

## When

*כְּשֶׁ* /kshe/ is the everyday "when". It is a prefix, written onto the front
of the next word, and it takes a full clause.

::: examples
כְּשֶׁהִגַּעְתִּי הַבַּיְתָה, כּוּלָּם כְּבָר יָשְׁנוּ || when I got home, everyone was already asleep
כְּשֶׁאֲנִי עָיֵיף אֲנִי לֹא מְדַבֵּר הַרְבֵּה || when I am tired I do not talk much
:::

*כַּאֲשֶׁר* /ka'asher/ is the same word in a suit. Identical meaning, identical
grammar, restricted to writing and to speeches. Nobody says it over coffee.

## The tense inside

::: rule
A Hebrew time clause takes the tense the event actually has. It does not
borrow a tense from the main clause the way English does.
:::

English says "I will call you when I **arrive**", with a present tense doing
duty for a future. Hebrew refuses. If the arriving has not happened, the verb
is in the future.

::: examples
כְּשֶׁתָּבוֹא, נְדַבֵּר || when you come, we will talk
אֶתְקַשֵּׁר אֵלֶיךָ כְּשֶׁאַגִּיעַ || I will call you when I arrive
כְּשֶׁיִּהְיֶה לִי זְמַן, אֶקְרָא אֶת זֶה || when I have time, I will read it
נְדַבֵּר אַחֲרֵי שֶׁתַּחְזוֹר || we will talk after you get back
:::

::: warning
*כְּשֶׁאֲנִי מַגִּיעַ, אֶתְקַשֵּׁר* is the mistake, and it is the single most
audible one an English speaker makes in this area. The present tense there
means "whenever I arrive, as a habit". For one future arrival, it has to be
*כְּשֶׁאַגִּיעַ*.
:::

The same holds for *אִם*. Hebrew puts the future in both halves:
*אִם יֵרֵד גֶּשֶׁם, נִישָּׁאֵר בַּבַּיִת*, if it rains we will stay home.

## Before and after

This pair splits by what follows, and the split is strict.

::: rule
*לִפְנֵי* and *אַחֲרֵי* take a noun. *לִפְנֵי שֶׁ* and *אַחֲרֵי שֶׁ* take a
clause with a conjugated verb. Neither takes an infinitive.
:::

::: examples
לִפְנֵי הָאֲרוּחָה || before the meal
לִפְנֵי שֶׁאָכַלְנוּ || before we ate
אַחֲרֵי הַשִּׁיעוּר || after the lesson
אַחֲרֵי שֶׁהִסְתַּיֵּים הַשִּׁיעוּר || after the lesson finished
:::

::: warning
*לִפְנֵי לֶאֱכוֹל* is not Hebrew. English "before eating" has no direct
equivalent: you either name the event as a noun, *לִפְנֵי הָאֲרוּחָה*, or you
conjugate it, *לִפְנֵי שֶׁאוֹכְלִים*. The same for *אַחֲרֵי*. This is worth
checking every time you write it, because the wrong version sounds
translated.
:::

## The full set

| Connector | Takes | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| כְּשֶׁ | a clause | when |
| כַּאֲשֶׁר | a clause | when, formal |
| לִפְנֵי | a noun | before |
| לִפְנֵי שֶׁ | a clause | before |
| אַחֲרֵי | a noun | after |
| אַחֲרֵי שֶׁ | a clause | after |
| בִּזְמַן שֶׁ | a clause | while, at the same time as |
| בְּעוֹד שֶׁ | a clause | while, with a flavour of contrast |
| מֵאָז | a noun | since |
| מֵאָז שֶׁ | a clause | since |
| מִשֶּׁ | a clause | from the moment that, literary |
| עַד | a noun | until |
| עַד שֶׁ | a clause | until |
| כָּל עוֹד | a clause | as long as |
| בְּכָל פַּעַם שֶׁ | a clause | every time that |
| בְּמִקְרֶה שֶׁ | a clause | in case, in the event that |
| בִּתְנַאי שֶׁ | a clause | provided that |
| אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן | a clause | unless |

::: examples
בִּזְמַן שֶׁדִּיבַּרְנוּ, הוּא כָּתַב הַכֹּל || while we were talking, he wrote everything down
מֵאָז שֶׁעָבַרְנוּ לְתֵל אָבִיב אֲנַחְנוּ הוֹלְכִים לַיָּם כָּל שַׁבָּת || since we moved to Tel Aviv we go to the sea every Saturday
חִיכִּיתִי עַד שֶׁהִיא הִגִּיעָה || I waited until she arrived
אַל תֵּלֵךְ עַד שֶׁאֶחְזוֹר || do not leave until I get back
כָּל עוֹד יֵשׁ לָנוּ זְמַן, אֵין מָה לִדְאוֹג || as long as we have time, there is nothing to worry about
בְּכָל פַּעַם שֶׁאֲנִי שׁוֹמֵעַ אֶת הַשִּׁיר הַזֶּה אֲנִי נִזְכָּר בָּהּ || every time I hear this song I think of her
בְּמִקְרֶה שֶׁתְּאַחֵר, תִּתְקַשֵּׁר אֵלַיי || in case you are late, call me
נַסְכִּים בִּתְנַאי שֶׁתַּחְתְּמוּ הַיּוֹם || we will agree provided that you sign today
לֹא נֵצֵא אֶלָּא אִם כֵּן מֶזֶג הָאֲוִויר יִשְׁתַּפֵּר || we will not go out unless the weather improves
:::

::: note
*עַד שֶׁ* takes the future for something still to come, *עַד שֶׁאֶחְזוֹר*, and
the past for something that already happened, *עַד שֶׁהִיא הִגִּיעָה*. That is
the general rule at work again: the verb takes its own tense.
:::

::: note
*בְּעוֹד שֶׁ* and *בִּזְמַן שֶׁ* both translate "while", but they are not
interchangeable. *בִּזְמַן שֶׁ* is neutrally simultaneous. *בְּעוֹד שֶׁ* sets
two things against each other and is closer to "whereas": *הוּא עָבַד,
בְּעוֹד שֶׁהִיא נָחָה*. Chapter 5 comes back to it.
:::

::: tip
*מֵאָז*, *לִפְנֵי*, *אַחֲרֵי* and *עַד* all work in two modes. Bare, they are
prepositions and want a noun. With *שֶׁ* glued on, they are conjunctions and
want a verb. If you can say "the meal", drop the *שֶׁ*; if you can say "we
ate", keep it.
:::

::: exercise Time and tense
"when I arrive, I will call" || *כְּשֶׁאַגִּיעַ, אֶתְקַשֵּׁר* || the arrival is in the future, so the verb is
"before the meal" || *לִפְנֵי הָאֲרוּחָה* || a bare noun, no *שֶׁ*
"before we ate" || *לִפְנֵי שֶׁאָכַלְנוּ* || a clause, so *שֶׁ* is obligatory
What is wrong with *לִפְנֵי לֶאֱכוֹל*? || *לִפְנֵי* never takes an infinitive || use a noun or *לִפְנֵי שֶׁ* plus a verb
"I waited until she arrived" || *חִיכִּיתִי עַד שֶׁהִיא הִגִּיעָה* || past event, past verb
"wait until I get back" || *חַכֵּה עַד שֶׁאֶחְזוֹר* || future event, future verb
"since we moved to Haifa" || *מֵאָז שֶׁעָבַרְנוּ לְחֵיפָה* || a clause, so *מֵאָז שֶׁ*
Formal twin of *כְּשֶׁ*? || *כַּאֲשֶׁר* || same grammar, written register only
:::
