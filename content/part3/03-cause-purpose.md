# Cause and purpose

Three questions hang off a single event: why did it happen, what followed from
it, and what was it for. Hebrew answers each with its own set of connectors,
and the sets do not overlap. The commonest mistake at this level is not
choosing the wrong word for "because" but reaching for the cause words when
the sentence is actually about purpose.

## Why it happened

The division is mechanical. One group takes a noun, the other takes a clause.

| Connector | Takes | Register |
|---|---|---|
| בִּגְלַל | a noun | neutral |
| כִּי | a clause | neutral, very common |
| מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ | a clause | neutral to careful |
| מִשּׁוּם שֶׁ | a clause | written |
| מֵאַחַר שֶׁ | a clause | formal |
| הוֹאִיל וְ | a clause | legal, very formal |
| עֵקֶב | a noun | written |
| בְּשֶׁל | a noun | written |

::: examples
לֹא הִגַּעְנוּ בִּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם || we did not make it because of the rain
לֹא הִגַּעְנוּ כִּי יָרַד גֶּשֶׁם || we did not make it because it rained
לֹא הִגַּעְנוּ מִפְּנֵי שֶׁיָּרַד גֶּשֶׁם || the same, one notch up
הַטִּיסָה בּוּטְּלָה עֵקֶב מֶזֶג הָאֲוִויר || the flight was cancelled owing to the weather
הַכְּבִישׁ נִסְגַּר בְּשֶׁל תְּאוּנָה || the road was closed due to an accident
מֵאַחַר שֶׁלֹּא קִיבַּלְנוּ תְּשׁוּבָה, פָּנִינוּ שׁוּב || since we received no answer, we applied again
:::

::: warning
*בִּגְלַל שֶׁ* is everywhere in speech and the Academy has been objecting to it
for eighty years without effect. *בִּגְלַל שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה זְמַן* is what people
say; *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה זְמַן* is what they write. Use *בִּגְלַל* with a
noun and *כִּי* or *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ* with a clause and you are safe in both
directions.
:::

::: note
*בִּגְלַל* carries a faint negative colouring, like English "because of" in
"because of him we were late". For a good cause, *בִּזְכוּת* /bizkhut/ is the
matching word: *בִּזְכוּתָהּ הִצְלַחְנוּ*, thanks to her we succeeded.
:::

## What followed from it

The result connectors join two full sentences, and all of them come at the
front of the second one.

::: examples
יָרַד גֶּשֶׁם, לָכֵן לֹא הִגַּעְנוּ || it rained, so we did not make it
הָיִיתִי חוֹלֶה וְלָכֵן לֹא בָּאתִי לָעֲבוֹדָה || I was ill and therefore did not come in
הַמִּכְרָז בּוּטַּל, וּכְתוֹצָאָה מִכָּךְ נִדְחָה הַפְּרוֹיֶקְט || the tender was cancelled, and as a result the project was postponed
מִשּׁוּם כָּךְ הֶחְלַטְנוּ לְחַכּוֹת || for that reason we decided to wait
:::

Notice the pair: *מִשּׁוּם שֶׁ* is "because", *מִשּׁוּם כָּךְ* is "therefore".
One letter of difference in speech, opposite directions of the arrow.

## What it was for

Purpose is where English speakers lose sentences, because English uses "to"
for both halves of the distinction and Hebrew does not.

::: rule
*כְּדֵי* plus an infinitive when the two clauses have the same subject.
*כְּדֵי שֶׁ* plus a **future** verb when the subject changes.
:::

::: examples
בָּאתִי כְּדֵי לִלְמוֹד || I came in order to study
בָּאתִי כְּדֵי שֶׁתִּלְמַד || I came so that you would study
הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת קָשֶׁה כְּדֵי לְהַצְלִיחַ || she works hard in order to succeed
הִיא עוֹבֶדֶת קָשֶׁה כְּדֵי שֶׁהַיְלָדִים יַצְלִיחוּ || she works hard so that the children will succeed
:::

In the first sentence of each pair, the person who comes is the person who
studies, so the second verb has nothing to mark and stays an infinitive. In
the second, a new subject appears, the infinitive has no room for it, and
Hebrew switches to a finite verb in the future.

::: warning
*בָּאתִי כְּדֵי אַתָּה לִלְמוֹד* and *בָּאתִי כְּדֵי שֶׁאַתָּה לוֹמֵד* are both
wrong. After *כְּדֵי שֶׁ* the verb is in the future, always, whatever tense the
main clause is in: *בָּאתִי אֶתְמוֹל כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין*, I came yesterday so
that you would understand.
:::

The formal alternatives behave the same way.

| Connector | Takes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| כְּדֵי | infinitive | the everyday one |
| כְּדֵי שֶׁ | future verb | subject changes |
| עַל מְנָת | infinitive | careful writing |
| עַל מְנָת שֶׁ | future verb | careful writing |
| לְמַעַן | noun, or future with שֶׁ | formal, slightly elevated |
| בִּשְׁבִיל | noun, or infinitive in speech | colloquial |

::: examples
עָשִׂינוּ זֹאת לְמַעַן הַיְלָדִים || we did it for the children's sake
עַל מְנָת לְהַגִּישׁ בַּקָּשָׁה יֵשׁ לְמַלֵּא טוֹפֶס || in order to submit a request one must fill in a form
קָנִיתִי אֶת זֶה בִּשְׁבִיל אַבָּא || I bought this for dad
:::

::: note
*בִּשְׁבִיל* plus an infinitive, *בִּשְׁבִיל לְהַסְבִּיר*, is ordinary speech
and is edited out of writing in favour of *כְּדֵי לְהַסְבִּיר*. *בִּשְׁבִיל*
plus a noun is fine at every level.
:::

## So that it does not happen

The negative purpose clause keeps the same same-subject rule and simply adds
*לֹא*.

::: examples
יָצָאתִי מוּקְדָּם כְּדֵי לֹא לְאַחֵר || I left early so as not to be late
יָצָאתִי מוּקְדָּם שֶׁלֹּא לְאַחֵר || the same, in careful writing
דִּיבַּרְתִּי בְּשֶׁקֶט כְּדֵי שֶׁהַיֶּלֶד לֹא יִתְעוֹרֵר || I spoke quietly so the child would not wake
שָׂמְתִּי תִּזְכּוֹרֶת שֶׁלֹּא אֶשְׁכַּח || I set a reminder so I would not forget
:::

::: note
*פֶּן* /pen/ is the old word for "lest". It takes a future verb and survives
only in fixed literary phrases: *פֶּן יִקְרֶה דָּבָר*, lest something happen.
Recognise it; do not use it.
:::

::: exercise Cause, result, purpose
"because of the rain" || *בִּגְלַל הַגֶּשֶׁם* || *בִּגְלַל* takes a noun
"because it rained" || *כִּי יָרַד גֶּשֶׁם* || a clause needs *כִּי* or *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁ*
Rewrite *בִּגְלַל שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה זְמַן* for a formal letter || *מִפְּנֵי שֶׁלֹּא הָיָה זְמַן* || *בִּגְלַל שֶׁ* is spoken only
"I came in order to study" || *בָּאתִי כְּדֵי לִלְמוֹד* || same subject, so an infinitive
"I came so that you would study" || *בָּאתִי כְּדֵי שֶׁתִּלְמַד* || the subject changes, so *שֶׁ* plus a future verb
What tense goes after *כְּדֵי שֶׁ*? || the future, whatever the main clause is || *בָּאתִי אֶתְמוֹל כְּדֵי שֶׁתָּבִין*
"It rained, so we stayed home" || *יָרַד גֶּשֶׁם, לָכֵן נִשְׁאַרְנוּ בַּבַּיִת* || *לָכֵן* opens the second clause
"so that the child would not wake" || *כְּדֵי שֶׁהַיֶּלֶד לֹא יִתְעוֹרֵר* || *לֹא* goes in front of the future verb
:::
