# Nif'al against hitpa'el

These are the two binyanim that will not sit still. Both can translate an
English passive, both can translate an English intransitive, and both appear
on roots you already know in pi'el or hif'il. Learners who have got this far
usually understand each one separately and still pick the wrong one under
pressure. This chapter is about the line between them.

## What nif'al does

Three jobs, and only the first is a passive.

**1. The passive of pa'al.** Whatever pa'al does to an object, nif'al does to
the same word as a subject.

::: examples
הַמְּנַהֵל כָּתַב אֶת הַמִּכְתָּב || the manager wrote the letter
הַמִּכְתָּב נִכְתַּב עַל יְדֵי הַמְּנַהֵל || the letter was written by the manager
סָגְרוּ אֶת הַחֲנוּת || they closed the shop
הַחֲנוּת נִסְגְּרָה בְּשֶׁבַע || the shop was closed at seven
:::

*עַל יְדֵי* /al yedei/ is the phrase that names the agent, and Hebrew uses it
far less than English uses "by". A nif'al sentence usually just leaves the
agent out.

::: note
The present and the past of nif'al differ by one vowel: *נִכְתָּב* with a
kamatz is "is being written", *נִכְתַּב* with a patach is "was written".
Unpointed they are the same three letters, נכתב, and only the sentence tells
you which.
:::

**2. A plain intransitive with no passive sense at all.** These are the most
frequent nif'al verbs in the language and not one of them means "was done by
someone".

::: examples
נִכְנַסְתִּי לַחֶדֶר || I went into the room
נִשְׁאַרְנוּ בַּבַּיִת || we stayed home
הַסֶּרֶט נִגְמַר || the film ended
הַמִּסְעָדָה נִמְצֵאת בַּמֶּרְכָּז || the restaurant is located in the centre
הוּא נֶעֱלַם || he disappeared
:::

**3. A state.** *נִרְאֶה* /nir'e/ and *נִשְׁמַע* /nishma/ are the two that
matter, and they behave like English "look" and "sound".

::: examples
אַתָּה נִרְאֶה עָיֵיף || you look tired
זֶה נִשְׁמַע טוֹב || that sounds good
נִרְאֶה לִי שֶׁהוּא צוֹדֵק || it seems to me that he is right
:::

## What hitpa'el does

Four jobs, and all four have the subject involved in the action rather than
receiving it from outside.

**1. Reflexive: doing it to yourself.**

::: examples
הִתְלַבַּשְׁתִּי מַהֵר || I got dressed quickly
הוּא הִתְרַחֵץ וְיָצָא || he washed and went out
הִיא הִסְתָּרְקָה מוּל הַמַּרְאָה || she combed her hair at the mirror
:::

**2. Reciprocal: doing it to each other.** This is the job no other binyan
does, and it always has a plural subject.

::: examples
הֵם הִתְכַּתְּבוּ בְּמֶשֶׁךְ שָׁנִים || they corresponded for years
הֵם הִתְנַשְּׁקוּ בְּסוֹף הַסֶּרֶט || they kissed at the end of the film
הַהוֹרִים שֶׁלִּי הִתְגָּרְשׁוּ || my parents got divorced
הִתְוַוכַּחְנוּ כָּל הָעֶרֶב || we argued all evening
:::

**3. A change of state: becoming something.**

::: examples
הִתְעַיַּיפְתִּי || I got tired
הִתְרַגַּשְׁתִּי מְאוֹד || I was very moved
הַמַּצָּב הִתְקַשָּׁה || the situation got harder
הוּא הִתְאַהֵב בָּהּ || he fell in love with her
:::

**4. A middle voice, where the subject is not really acting and nobody else
is either.** The thing simply undergoes the change on its own.

::: examples
הַמַּצָּב הִשְׁתַּנָּה || the situation changed
הָעִיר הִתְפַּתְּחָה מְאוֹד || the city developed a great deal
הַצִּינּוֹר הִתְפּוֹצֵץ || the pipe burst
:::

::: rule
If you could add "by someone" in English, Hebrew wants nif'al. If the subject
is doing it to itself, or two subjects are doing it to each other, Hebrew
wants hitpa'el.
:::

## One root, both binyanim

Where a root has a nif'al and a hitpa'el, they never mean the same thing.

| Root | Nif'al | Hitpa'el |
|---|---|---|
| כ-ת-ב | נִכְתַּב it was written | הִתְכַּתֵּב he corresponded |
| כ-נ-ס | נִכְנַס he went in | הִתְכַּנֵּס it convened |
| ג-ל-ה | נִגְלָה it was revealed | הִתְגַּלָּה it came to light |
| ק-שׁ-ר | נִקְשַׁר he grew attached | הִתְקַשֵּׁר he phoned, he got in touch |
| שׁ-מ-ר | נִשְׁמַר it was kept | הִשְׁתַּמֵּר it was preserved, it kept |
| ר-א-ה | נִרְאֶה it looks, it seems | הִתְרָאוּ they saw one another |

Read the column headings as the difference. *נִגְלָה* has somebody doing the
revealing behind it; *הִתְגַּלָּה* has the thing surfacing by itself.
*נִקְשַׁר* happens to you; *הִתְקַשֵּׁר* is something you do.

## The pairs across binyanim

The other confusion is not nif'al against hitpa'el but nif'al against hif'il,
because hif'il supplies the transitive partner that nif'al lacks.

::: examples
נִזְכַּרְתִּי בַּשֵּׁם שֶׁלּוֹ || I remembered his name, it came back to me
הִזְכַּרְתִּי לוֹ אֶת הַפְּגִישָׁה || I reminded him of the meeting
נִכְנַסְתִּי לַמִּשְׂרָד || I went into the office
הִכְנַסְתִּי אֶת הַתִּיק לַמִּשְׂרָד || I brought the bag into the office
:::

::: rule
Nif'al is intransitive and takes no *אֶת*. Hif'il is transitive and normally
does. If the sentence has a direct object, the nif'al form is the wrong one.
:::

::: warning
*הִשְׁתַּמֵּשׁ* /hishtamesh/ is hitpa'el and is neither reflexive nor
reciprocal, and *נִפְגַּשׁ* /nifgash/ is nif'al and is reciprocal:
*נִפְגַּשְׁנוּ בַּקָּפֶה*, we met at the café. Every generalisation in this
chapter has a few members who never got the memo. Use the rule to guess, then
check the word.
:::

::: tip
Under pressure, ask one question: is there somebody outside the sentence doing
this? Yes, and you left them out, means nif'al. No, the subject is doing it to
itself or the two of them are doing it to each other, means hitpa'el.
:::

::: exercise Pick the binyan
"The letter was written" || *הַמִּכְתָּב נִכְתַּב* || an agent could be added, so nif'al
"They wrote to each other" || *הֵם הִתְכַּתְּבוּ* || reciprocal, so hitpa'el
"I got dressed" || *הִתְלַבַּשְׁתִּי* || the subject acts on itself
"I went into the room" || *נִכְנַסְתִּי לַחֶדֶר* || nif'al with no passive sense at all
"I brought the chair into the room" || *הִכְנַסְתִּי אֶת הַכִּיסֵּא לַחֶדֶר* || there is an object, so hif'il not nif'al
What is the difference between *נִזְכַּרְתִּי* and *הִזְכַּרְתִּי*? || the first is "I remembered", the second is "I reminded someone" || nif'al is intransitive, hif'il takes an object
Why is *נִרְאֶה* not a passive? || it describes a state, "looks" or "seems", not something done to the subject || the same for *נִשְׁמַע*
"The situation changed" || *הַמַּצָּב הִשְׁתַּנָּה* || middle voice: nobody changed it, it changed
:::
