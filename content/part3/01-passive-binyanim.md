# The passive binyanim

Part II chapter 6 named all seven binyanim and then walked past two of them.
This chapter is those two. *פּוּעַל* /pu'al/ is the passive of pi'el,
*הוּפְעַל* /huf'al/ is the passive of hif'il, and between them they cover a
slice of Hebrew you will read constantly and say almost never.

## Three features that give them away

::: rule
Both binyanim have a u vowel in the first syllable, no infinitive, and no
imperative. Nothing else in the verb system looks like that.
:::

The u vowel is the whole signature. In the older pointing it is a kubutz,
*דֻּבַּר*; in full spelling it is a shuruk, *דּוּבַּר* /dubar/, and this course
writes the second. Once you see a verb beginning *דּוּ*, *סוּ*, *בּוּ*, *הוּ*
or *מוּ*, you are looking at a passive.

The missing infinitive is not an accident. An infinitive exists to sit after
*רוֹצֶה*, *צָרִיךְ* or *יָכוֹל*, and all three need somebody who wants, must or
can. A form whose subject is by definition not doing anything has nobody to
fill that slot. So "the room has to be prepared" is not built from huf'al at
all: *צָרִיךְ לְהָכִין אֶת הַחֶדֶר* /tzarikh lehakhin/, one has to prepare the
room.

## Pu'al, on the root ד-ב-ר

| | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | מְדוּבָּר | דּוּבַּר | יְדוּבַּר |
| feminine singular | מְדוּבֶּרֶת | דּוּבְּרָה | תְּדוּבַּר |
| plural | מְדוּבָּרִים, מְדוּבָּרוֹת | דּוּבְּרוּ | יְדוּבְּרוּ |

The stem never changes: *דּוּבַּר* under all three tenses, with the past taking
nothing in front of it, the present taking *מְ* and the future taking the
ordinary future prefixes.

## Huf'al, on the root ס-ב-ר

| | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | מוּסְבָּר | הוּסְבַּר | יוּסְבַּר |
| feminine singular | מוּסְבֶּרֶת | הוּסְבְּרָה | תּוּסְבַּר |
| plural | מוּסְבָּרִים, מוּסְבָּרוֹת | הוּסְבְּרוּ | יוּסְבְּרוּ |

::: note
The *ה* of the past is dropped in the present and the future, exactly as it is
in hif'il: *הִסְבִּיר*, *מַסְבִּיר*, *יַסְבִּיר*, and here *הוּסְבַּר*,
*מוּסְבָּר*, *יוּסְבַּר*. The u vowel is what tells the two apart.
:::

First and second person forms exist (*דּוּבַּרְתִּי*, *הוּסְבַּרְתָּ*) and are
close to unusable. Passives are overwhelmingly third person, because the whole
point is that nobody is being named.

## The verbs that actually turn up

The list of pu'al and huf'al verbs in live circulation is short enough to
learn as vocabulary.

| Verb | Binyan | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| הוּזְמַן | huf'al | was invited, was ordered, was booked |
| הוּחְלַט | huf'al | it was decided |
| הוּכַן | huf'al | was prepared |
| סוּדַּר | pu'al | was arranged, was sorted out |
| בּוּטַּל | pu'al | was cancelled |
| טוּפַּל | pu'al | was handled, was dealt with |

::: examples
הוּחְלַט לִסְגּוֹר אֶת הַכְּבִישׁ בְּשָׁעָה שֶׁבַע || it was decided to close the road at seven
הַטִּיסָה בּוּטְּלָה בִּגְלַל מֶזֶג הָאֲוִויר || the flight was cancelled because of the weather
הָאוּלָם הוּזְמַן מֵרֹאשׁ || the hall was booked in advance
הַבַּקָּשָׁה שֶׁלְּךָ טוּפְּלָה || your request has been dealt with
הַכֹּל כְּבָר סוּדַּר || everything has already been sorted
:::

*הוּזְמַנְתִּי* /huzmanti/ is the one first-person passive an Israeli says
without flinching: *הוּזְמַנְתִּי לַחֲתוּנָה*, I was invited to the wedding.

## What speech does instead

Almost everywhere else, spoken Hebrew refuses both binyanim and uses one of
two escapes.

::: rule
The everyday Hebrew passive is an active verb in the third person plural with
no subject at all. *אָמְרוּ לִי* is literally "they told me" and means "I was
told", with no claim that anyone in particular did the telling.
:::

::: examples
אָמְרוּ לִי שֶׁהַמִּשְׂרָד סָגוּר || I was told the office is closed
גָּנְבוּ לִי אֶת הָאוֹפַנַּיִים || my bike was stolen
בִּיטְּלוּ אֶת הַטִּיסָה || the flight was cancelled
עוֹד לֹא הֶחְלִיטוּ || it has not been decided yet
:::

The second escape is nif'al, which is a living binyan and takes over wherever
a pa'al verb has a passive to express: *נִסְגַּר* it was closed, *נִשְׁלַח*
it was sent, *נִקְבַּע* it was set, *נִבְחַר* he was chosen.

## Where you will meet them

Three places, and they are all written:

- **Official language.** *הַתַּשְׁלוּם בּוּטַּל*, the payment has been
  cancelled. *הַתִּיק שֶׁלְּךָ טוּפַּל*, your file has been handled.
- **News.** *הוּחְלַט כִּי הַדִּיּוּן יִידָּחֶה*, it was decided that the
  hearing would be postponed. A headline needs a verb with no subject, and
  these two supply it.
- **Academic prose.** *כְּפִי שֶׁהוּסְבַּר לְעֵיל*, as was explained above.

::: warning
Do not manufacture your own pu'al and huf'al forms to sound advanced. An
invented *הוּלְמַד* for "was taught" is not Hebrew, and even correct forms
sound like a tax letter in conversation.
:::

## The useful residue

The most valuable thing these two binyanim left behind is a set of present
participles that stopped being verbs and became ordinary adjectives. You have
been using some of them since Part I without knowing where they came from.

::: examples
הַחֶדֶר מְסוּדָּר || the room is tidy
אֲנִי מְבוּלְבָּל || I am confused
הָאוֹכֶל מוּכָן || the food is ready
אֲנִי מְעוּנְיָין בַּמִּשְׂרָה || I am interested in the position
הַשִּׁיר הַזֶּה מְפוּרְסָם מְאוֹד || that song is very famous
זֶה קְצָת מוּגְזָם || that is a bit over the top
:::

They inflect like any adjective: *מְסוּדֶּרֶת*, *מְסוּדָּרִים*,
*מְסוּדָּרוֹת*.

::: tip
A word beginning *מְ* or *מוּ* with a u vowel right after it is a passive
participle: *מְדוּבָּר*, *מוּכָן*, *מוּכָּר*, *מְקוּבָּל*. Ask whether it is
functioning as a verb or as an adjective, and in modern Hebrew the answer is
usually the adjective.
:::

::: exercise Passive or not
Why is there no infinitive in pu'al? || the binyan has no acting subject, and an infinitive needs one || say it with an active verb instead
Turn *דִּיבְּרוּ עַל זֶה* into pu'al || *דוּבַּר עַל זֶה* || it was discussed
Make *הִסְבִּיר* passive, past, masculine singular || *הוּסְבַּר* || hif'il pairs with huf'al
Say "the flight was cancelled" the way an Israeli would || *בִּיטְּלוּ אֶת הַטִּיסָה* || the subjectless third person plural
Say the same in the language of an airline notice || *הַטִּיסָה בּוּטְּלָה* || pu'al belongs to written notices
"The room has to be prepared" || *צָרִיךְ לְהָכִין אֶת הַחֶדֶר* || no huf'al infinitive exists, so the sentence turns active
What binyan is *מוּכָן*, and what is it doing? || huf'al participle, working as the adjective "ready" || the most common survivor of the two
What does *אָמְרוּ לִי* mean? || I was told || literally "they said to me", with no they
:::
