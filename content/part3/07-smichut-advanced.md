# Smichut in depth

Part I chapter 25 gave you the shape: two nouns pushed together, the first one
changed, the article on the second. *בֵּית סֵפֶר*, *עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד*. That
was enough to read a sign. This chapter is the rest of it, and the rest is
where Hebrew stops feeling like English with different words.

## The plural construct

Singular smikhut is mostly memorisation. The plural is a rule, and it is the
most useful construct rule in the language.

::: rule
A masculine plural ending in *ִים* becomes *ֵי* in the construct state. The ם
is dropped.
:::

| Free form | Construct | In use |
|---|---|---|
| סְפָרִים | סִפְרֵי | סִפְרֵי לִימּוּד textbooks |
| בָּתִּים | בָּתֵּי | בָּתֵּי סֵפֶר schools |
| אֲנָשִׁים | אַנְשֵׁי | אַנְשֵׁי עֲסָקִים businesspeople |
| בָּנִים | בְּנֵי | בְּנֵי אָדָם human beings |
| חַיִּים | חַיֵּי | חַיֵּי אָדָם human life |
| דְּבָרִים | דִּבְרֵי | דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים chronicles |
| עוֹרְכִים | עוֹרְכֵי | עוֹרְכֵי דִּין lawyers |

The vowels inside the word reduce at the same time, exactly as they do in the
plural itself: *סֵפֶר*, *סְפָרִים*, *סִפְרֵי*.

Feminine plurals are the easy half. The *וֹת* ending does not change at all.

::: examples
מִשְׁפְּחוֹת הָעִיר || the families of the city
עוּגוֹת שׁוֹקוֹלָד || chocolate cakes
חֲנוּיוֹת בְּגָדִים || clothes shops
תְּמוּנוֹת הַיְלָדִים || the children's photographs
:::

The ending stays; the stem vowels may still reduce, as *מִשְׁפָּחוֹת* to
*מִשְׁפְּחוֹת*. In speech people often leave the vowels alone and nobody
minds.

## Chains of three

A construct noun can govern another construct noun. Every link except the last
is in the construct state.

::: examples
רֹאשׁ מֶמְשֶׁלֶת יִשְׂרָאֵל || the Prime Minister of Israel
מְנַהֵל בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר || the headmaster of the school
מִשְׂרַד רֹאשׁ הַמֶּמְשָׁלָה || the Prime Minister's Office
מְסִיבַּת יוֹם הַהוּלֶדֶת שֶׁל דָּנָה || Dana's birthday party
:::

Read them from the right and they unpack neatly: head, of the government, of
Israel. Newspaper Hebrew stacks these three and four deep, which is what
chapter 20 of Part II was warning you about.

## Where the article goes

::: rule
One *הַ* per chain, on the last noun only, and it makes the whole chain
definite.
:::

::: examples
בֵּית סֵפֶר || a school
בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר || the school
בָּתֵּי סֵפֶר || schools
בָּתֵּי הַסֵּפֶר || the schools
:::

Note *בָּתֵּי הַסֵּפֶר*, not *בָּתֵּי הַסְּפָרִים*. Only the first noun of the
chain is pluralised; the second is frozen.

::: warning
*הַבֵּית סֵפֶר* is the mistake, and it is a common one, because the chain
feels like one word and the article wants to go at the front. It cannot. In
casual speech you will hear *הַבֵּית חוֹלִים* from Israelis too; it is still
not correct.
:::

## Adding an adjective

The adjective goes after the whole chain, and it agrees with whichever noun it
describes. Since the first noun is the head, it usually agrees with that one.

::: examples
בֵּית סֵפֶר גָּדוֹל || a big school
בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר הַגָּדוֹל || the big school
עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד טְעִימָה || a tasty chocolate cake
בָּתֵּי הַסֵּפֶר הַחֲדָשִׁים || the new schools
:::

*טְעִימָה* is feminine because *עוּגָה* is, not because *שׁוֹקוֹלָד* is
anything. When the adjective is meant to describe the second noun instead, the
agreement is the only clue, and if the two nouns share a gender the phrase is
genuinely ambiguous. That is one of the reasons Hebrew often abandons smikhut.

## The three phrases to keep apart

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *בַּיִת גָּדוֹל* | a big house |
| *בֵּית סֵפֶר גָּדוֹל* | a big school |
| *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר הַגָּדוֹל* | the big school |
| *הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל* | the big house |

*בַּיִת* and *בֵּית* differ by one vowel and mean different things in a
sentence: the second is not a house, it is the front half of a compound and
cannot stand alone. If you hear *בֵּית* and no second noun follows, you
misheard.

## When Hebrew prefers של

Smikhut is not the general possessive. It is a compound-forming device, and
using it for ordinary ownership sounds archaic or wrong.

::: rule
Use smikhut for a fixed compound: a kind of thing. Use *שֶׁל* for possession:
whose thing it is.
:::

Three situations force *שֶׁל*:

1. **Ordinary possession.** *הַמְּכוֹנִית שֶׁל אַבָּא*, dad's car, not
   *מְכוֹנִית אַבָּא*.
2. **Either noun is modified.** An adjective cannot get inside a chain, so
   *הַסֵּפֶר הֶחָדָשׁ שֶׁל הַמּוֹרֶה*, the teacher's new book, has no smikhut
   version.
3. **The compound is not fixed.** If the two nouns do not name a recognised
   category, Hebrew does not weld them.

| Must be smikhut | | Must be שֶׁל | |
|---|---|---|---|
| בֵּית סֵפֶר | school | הַמְּכוֹנִית שֶׁל אַבָּא | dad's car |
| בֵּית חוֹלִים | hospital | הַבַּיִת שֶׁל הַשְּׁכֵנִים | the neighbours' house |
| בֵּית קָפֶה | café | הַתִּיק שֶׁל דָּנָה | Dana's bag |
| עוֹרֵךְ דִּין | lawyer | הַכֶּלֶב שֶׁל הַיַּלְדָּה | the girl's dog |
| יוֹם הוּלֶדֶת | birthday | הַצֶּבַע שֶׁל הַקִּיר | the colour of the wall |
| גַּן יְלָדִים | kindergarten | הָרַעְיוֹן שֶׁל רוֹנִית | Ronit's idea |
| תְּעוּדַת זֶהוּת | identity card | הַסֵּפֶר הֶחָדָשׁ שֶׁל הַמּוֹרֶה | the teacher's new book |
| תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּס | bus stop | הַדִּירָה שֶׁלָּנוּ | our flat |

::: note
Formal writing has a third option that combines both: *סִפְרוֹ שֶׁל
הַמּוֹרֶה*, literally "his book, of the teacher". You will meet it in
newspapers and in academic prose. It is never spoken.
:::

## Smikhut that turned into vocabulary

A very large part of the modern lexicon is a construct chain that stopped
being felt as two words. Some are still written apart and behave as one item:
*כַּדּוּר רֶגֶל*, *בֵּית מִשְׁפָּט*, *סֵדֶר יוֹם*. Others have fused on the
page.

::: examples
רַמְזוֹר || a traffic light: רֶמֶז plus אוֹר
קוֹלְנוֹעַ || cinema: קוֹל plus נוֹעַ
מַדְחוֹם || a thermometer: מַד plus חוֹם
מִגְדַּלּוֹר || a lighthouse: מִגְדָּל plus אוֹר
שְׁמַרְטַף || a babysitter: שׁוֹמֵר plus טַף
כַּדּוּרֶגֶל || football, written as one word
:::

::: tip
When you meet a long unfamiliar word, try splitting it. Half the time the two
halves are words you already have, and the meaning falls out without a
dictionary.
:::

::: exercise Build the chain
Plural construct of *סְפָרִים* || *סִפְרֵי* || *ִים* becomes *ֵי*
"schools" || *בָּתֵּי סֵפֶר* || only the first noun goes plural
"the schools" || *בָּתֵּי הַסֵּפֶר* || the article sits on the last noun only
What is wrong with *הַבֵּית סֵפֶר*? || the article cannot go on the first noun of a chain || it must be *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר*
"the big school" || *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר הַגָּדוֹל* || definite chain, so the adjective takes *הַ* too
"a big school" || *בֵּית סֵפֶר גָּדוֹל* || nothing is definite, so nothing takes *הַ*
"dad's car" || *הַמְּכוֹנִית שֶׁל אַבָּא* || ordinary possession, so *שֶׁל* and not smikhut
Why is there no smikhut for "the teacher's new book"? || an adjective cannot sit inside a construct chain || *הַסֵּפֶר הֶחָדָשׁ שֶׁל הַמּוֹרֶה*
:::
