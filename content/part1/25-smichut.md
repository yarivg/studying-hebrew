# Smichut: two nouns as one

English glues nouns together and hopes for the best: a bus station, a bathroom,
a chocolate cake. Hebrew does the same thing, with a name and a rule.
*סְמִיכוּת* /smikhut/ puts two nouns side by side with nothing between them,
and the pair behaves as a single word.

::: examples
בֵּית סֵפֶר || a school, literally "house of book"
עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד || a chocolate cake
תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּס || a bus stop
חֲדַר אַמְבַּטְיָה || a bathroom
אֲרוּחַת בּוֹקֶר || breakfast
יוֹם הוּלֶדֶת || a birthday
:::

There is no word for "of" in any of those. The second noun defines the first,
and the two are said as one unit with a single main stress:
*בֵּית סֵפֶר* /beit sefer/ comes out as one word.

## The first noun changes shape

This is what makes smikhut more than word order. The first noun goes into the
construct state, a shortened form that exists only in this position.

| Alone | In smikhut | |
|---|---|---|
| *בַּיִת* | *בֵּית* | house |
| *עוּגָה* | *עוּגַת* | cake |
| *אֲרוּחָה* | *אֲרוּחַת* | meal |
| *תַּחֲנָה* | *תַּחֲנַת* | station |
| *מִשְׁפָּחָה* | *מִשְׁפַּחַת* | family |
| *חֶדֶר* | *חֲדַר* | room |

::: rule
A feminine noun ending in *ָה* ends in *ַת* in smikhut. That one swap covers
most of the words you will meet.
:::

The masculine changes are less predictable. *בַּיִת* becomes *בֵּית*, *חֶדֶר*
becomes *חֲדַר*, and a great many masculine nouns do not change at all:
*יוֹם הוּלֶדֶת* /yom huledet/, *מִיץ תַּפּוּזִים*, orange juice, *סֵפֶר
לִימּוּד*, a textbook. Learn each compound as a piece of vocabulary and the
shapes will settle by themselves.

## The article goes on the second noun

Here is the rule that trips everyone, and the one worth memorising verbatim.

::: rule
To make a smikhut definite, put *הַ* on the SECOND noun. It makes the whole
pair definite, not just the second word.
:::

::: examples
בֵּית סֵפֶר || a school
בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר || the school
תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּס || a bus stop
תַּחֲנַת הָאוֹטוֹבּוּס || the bus stop
עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד || a chocolate cake
עוּגַת הַשּׁוֹקוֹלָד || the chocolate cake
:::

::: warning
*בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר* is "the school". It is not "a house of the school" and it is
not "the book's house". Read the *הַ* as belonging to both words at once.
*הַבֵּית סֵפֶר* is wrong and sounds badly wrong; the first noun can never take
the article.
:::

## An adjective on a pair

An adjective describing a smikhut cannot squeeze into the middle. It waits
until both nouns are finished, and then agrees with the FIRST one, because the
first noun is the thing being described.

::: examples
בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר הַגָּדוֹל || the big school
עוּגַת הַשּׁוֹקוֹלָד הַטְּעִימָה || the delicious chocolate cake
תַּחֲנַת הָאוֹטוֹבּוּס הַחֲדָשָׁה || the new bus stop
:::

*הַטְּעִימָה* is feminine because *עוּגָה* is feminine, not because
*שׁוֹקוֹלָד* is anything. And the definiteness rule of chapter 12 still
applies: the pair is definite, so the adjective takes *הַ* too.

::: note
The plural is marked on the first noun, which is the one carrying the meaning:
*בָּתֵּי סֵפֶר*, schools; *תַּחֲנוֹת אוֹטוֹבּוּס*, bus stops. The construct
plurals have their own shapes and they wait until Part III.
:::

## The everyday alternative: *שֶׁל*

Hebrew also has an ordinary possessive, *שֶׁל* /shel/, from chapter 17, and it
is the one you will use ninety per cent of the time.

::: examples
הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה || Dana's book
הַבַּיִת שֶׁל הַמִּשְׁפָּחָה || the family's house
הַמְּכוֹנִית שֶׁל אַבָּא || dad's car
:::

With *שֶׁל*, nothing changes shape and both nouns take their own article
normally. Compare the two structures on the same words:

| | |
|---|---|
| *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר* | the school: a fixed thing with a name |
| *הַבַּיִת שֶׁל הַסֵּפֶר* | the book's house: whatever that would be |

::: rule
Smikhut is for fixed compounds, the kind that would be one word or a set
phrase in English. *שֶׁל* is for ordinary possession: this thing belongs to
that person. When you are not sure, use *שֶׁל*. You will sound plain, never
wrong.
:::

Nobody invents a new smikhut on the fly, and nobody needs to. What you need is
to recognise one when it comes past, know that the *הַ* on the second word
makes the whole thing definite, and say *הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה* for everything
else.

::: exercise Join the nouns
Say "a bus stop" || *תַּחֲנַת אוֹטוֹבּוּס* || *תַּחֲנָה* becomes *תַּחֲנַת*
Say "the bus stop" || *תַּחֲנַת הָאוֹטוֹבּוּס* || the article goes on the second noun
Make *עוּגָה* into its smikhut form || *עוּגַת* || feminine *ָה* becomes *ַת*
Say "the school" || *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר* || never *הַבֵּית סֵפֶר*
Say "the big school" || *בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר הַגָּדוֹל* || the adjective follows both and agrees with *בַּיִת*
Why is it *עוּגַת הַשּׁוֹקוֹלָד הַטְּעִימָה*? || the adjective agrees with *עוּגָה*, which is feminine || the second noun does not control agreement
Say "Dana's book" || *הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל דָּנָה* || ordinary possession, so *שֶׁל*
When should a beginner choose *שֶׁל*? || whenever the compound is not a fixed, learned phrase || plain but correct
:::
