# Present tense when a letter goes missing

Chapter 19 gave you a clean frame and four roots that fit it. Now the awkward
truth: the verbs you will actually say in your first month almost all break
it. Want, buy, drink, do, see, live, get up, come, read. Every one of those
has a root letter that will not behave.

::: rule
The irregular verbs are not an advanced topic. They are the frequent ones.
That is exactly why they come immediately after the regular pattern instead of
being deferred.
:::

A root where one letter drops, hides or turns into a vowel is called a weak
root. Three families cover nearly everything a beginner needs.

## ל"ה: the third letter disappears

Roots whose third letter is *ה* are called *ל"ה* /lamed hey/, meaning "the
third slot is a hey". In the present tense that *ה* is simply not pronounced,
and the four forms end in a vowel.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *רוֹצֶה* | *רוֹצִים* |
| feminine | *רוֹצָה* | *רוֹצוֹת* |

*רוֹצֶה* /rotze/, to want, infinitive *לִרְצוֹת* /lirtzot/. The masculine
singular ends in *ֶה*, the feminine in *ָה*, and without nikud both are רוצה.

::: warning
The feminine is *רוֹצָה*, with a plain *ָה*. It is **not** *רוֹצֵאת*, which is
what the ear of a beginner keeps producing by analogy with *כּוֹתֶבֶת*. The
*ֶת* ending of chapter 19 belongs to sound roots only. Here the
missing third letter leaves nothing for it to attach to, so the word takes the
ordinary feminine *ָה* instead.
:::

::: examples
אֲנִי רוֹצֶה קָפֶה || I want a coffee (a man)
אֲנִי רוֹצָה קָפֶה || I want a coffee (a woman)
הֵם רוֹצִים לִלְמוֹד || they want to study
מָה אַתְּ רוֹצָה? || what do you want?
:::

Four more, all extremely common:

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לִקְנוֹת* | קוֹנֶה | קוֹנָה | קוֹנִים | קוֹנוֹת | buy |
| *לִשְׁתּוֹת* | שׁוֹתֶה | שׁוֹתָה | שׁוֹתִים | שׁוֹתוֹת | drink |
| *לַעֲשׂוֹת* | עוֹשֶׂה | עוֹשָׂה | עוֹשִׂים | עוֹשׂוֹת | do, make |
| *לִרְאוֹת* | רוֹאֶה | רוֹאָה | רוֹאִים | רוֹאוֹת | see |

Notice the infinitives: every *ל"ה* verb ends in *וֹת*, which is the giveaway.
*לִרְצוֹת*, *לִקְנוֹת*, *לִשְׁתּוֹת*, *לַעֲשׂוֹת*, *לִרְאוֹת*.

::: sound
*עוֹשֶׂה* is /'ose/ with a sin, not a shin. *רוֹאֶה* is /ro'e/, two syllables,
with the alef silent between them. Neither has a consonant where the third
root letter used to be.
:::

## ע"ו and ע"י: only two consonants left

Some roots have *ו* or *י* in the middle, and in the present tense that middle
letter vanishes into the vowel of the infinitive. What is left is a very short
word with only two consonants in it.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *גָּר* | *גָּרִים* |
| feminine | *גָּרָה* | *גָּרוֹת* |

*גָּר* /gar/, to live somewhere, infinitive *לָגוּר* /lagur/. The root is
*ג-ו-ר*, and you can see the *ו* in the infinitive and nowhere in the present.

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לָקוּם* | קָם | קָמָה | קָמִים | קָמוֹת | get up |
| *לָשִׂים* | שָׂם | שָׂמָה | שָׂמִים | שָׂמוֹת | put |
| *לָבוֹא* | בָּא | בָּאָה | בָּאִים | בָּאוֹת | come |
| *לָרוּץ* | רָץ | רָצָה | רָצִים | רָצוֹת | run |
| *לָשִׁיר* | שָׁר | שָׁרָה | שָׁרִים | שָׁרוֹת | sing |

Their infinitives all start with *לָ*, with a kamatz, where the sound roots
had *לִ*. That is another free clue.

::: warning
*גָּר*, *קָם*, *בָּא* and the rest look exactly like adjectives: one syllable,
kamatz, then *ָה* for the feminine and *ִים* for the plural. They are verbs.
*אֲנִי גָּר בְּתֵל אָבִיב* is "I live in Tel Aviv", a full sentence with a verb
in it, not "I am resident".
:::

::: examples
אֲנִי גָּר בִּירוּשָׁלַיִם || I live in Jerusalem
הִיא קָמָה מוּקְדָּם || she gets up early
הֵם בָּאִים הַיּוֹם || they are coming today
אַתְּ שָׂמָה אֶת הַסֵּפֶר עַל הַשּׁוּלְחָן || you are putting the book on the table
:::

::: note
*רָצָה* is "she runs", from *לָרוּץ*. *רוֹצָה* is "she wants", from *לִרְצוֹת*.
Unpointed they are רצה and רוצה, so the *ו* is the only thing telling them
apart on a page. In speech the difference is /ratza/ against /rotza/.
:::

## ל"א: the alef is there and silent

Roots ending in *א* are called *ל"א* /lamed alef/. The letter is written in
every form and pronounced in none of them. Crucially, these verbs keep the
*ֶת* feminine ending of chapter 19: they are not missing a letter, only a
sound.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *קוֹרֵא* | *קוֹרְאִים* |
| feminine | *קוֹרֵאת* | *קוֹרְאוֹת* |

*קוֹרֵא* /kore/, to read and also to call, infinitive *לִקְרוֹא* /likro/.

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לִמְצוֹא* | מוֹצֵא | מוֹצֵאת | מוֹצְאִים | מוֹצְאוֹת | find |
| *לָצֵאת* | יוֹצֵא | יוֹצֵאת | יוֹצְאִים | יוֹצְאוֹת | go out, exit |

::: examples
הוּא קוֹרֵא עִיתּוֹן || he is reading a newspaper
הִיא קוֹרֵאת סֵפֶר || she is reading a book
אֲנַחְנוּ מוֹצְאִים אֶת הַמַּפְתֵּחַ || we are finding the key
הֵם יוֹצְאִים בָּעֶרֶב || they go out in the evening
:::

::: tip
Feminine singular, in one line. Sound root and *ל"א*: *ֶת* or *ֵאת*. Missing
third letter (*ל"ה*) and two-consonant (*ע"ו*): plain *ָה*. Decide which family
the verb is in and the ending follows.
:::

::: exercise Weak roots in the present
Say "I want" (a woman) || *אֲנִי רוֹצָה* || plain *ָה*, never *רוֹצֵאת*
Say "she reads" || *הִיא קוֹרֵאת* || *ל"א* keeps the *ֶת* ending
Make *לָגוּר* feminine plural || *גָּרוֹת* || the *ו* of the root never appears in the present
Say "they are buying a house" || *הֵם קוֹנִים בַּיִת* || *ל"ה*, so the plural is *ִים* on a bare stem
What is the difference between *רָצָה* and *רוֹצָה*? || *רָצָה* is "runs", *רוֹצָה* is "wants" || one is *ע"ו*, the other *ל"ה*
Say "we are drinking water" || *אֲנַחְנוּ שׁוֹתִים מַיִם* || from *לִשְׁתּוֹת*
Which family does an infinitive ending in *וֹת* belong to? || *ל"ה* || *לִרְצוֹת*, *לִקְנוֹת*, *לִרְאוֹת*
Say "he is going out now" || *הוּא יוֹצֵא עַכְשָׁיו* || infinitive *לָצֵאת*
:::
