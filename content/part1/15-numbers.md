# Numbers 1 to 100

Hebrew has two complete sets of numbers from one to ten, one masculine and one
feminine. This is not a decorative detail. Choosing the wrong set is the
single most audible beginner's mistake in the language, more audible than a
bad *ר*, because numbers come up in every second sentence.

## The two sets

| | Feminine | Masculine |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | אַחַת /akhat/ | אֶחָד /ekhad/ |
| 2 | שְׁתַּיִים /shtayim/ | שְׁנַיִים /shnayim/ |
| 3 | שָׁלוֹשׁ /shalosh/ | שְׁלוֹשָׁה /shlosha/ |
| 4 | אַרְבַּע /arba/ | אַרְבָּעָה /arba'a/ |
| 5 | חָמֵשׁ /khamesh/ | חֲמִישָּׁה /khamisha/ |
| 6 | שֵׁשׁ /shesh/ | שִׁישָּׁה /shisha/ |
| 7 | שֶׁבַע /sheva/ | שִׁבְעָה /shiv'a/ |
| 8 | שְׁמוֹנֶה /shmone/ | שְׁמוֹנָה /shmona/ |
| 9 | תֵּשַׁע /tesha/ | תִּשְׁעָה /tish'a/ |
| 10 | עֶשֶׂר /eser/ | עֲשָׂרָה /asara/ |

Look at the shapes before you memorise anything. The masculine set is the one
carrying *ָה* on the end, which is the ending you learned as feminine in
chapter 9. That is not a misprint and there is no satisfying explanation for
it. The forms that look feminine are masculine, and the bare forms are
feminine.

::: rule
The feminine set is the default. It is what you use to count in the abstract:
phone numbers, bus numbers, house numbers, arithmetic, counting to ten out
loud. It is also the set that agrees with a feminine noun.
:::

::: examples
אוֹטוֹבּוּס אַרְבַּע || bus number four
חָמֵשׁ, שֵׁשׁ, שֶׁבַע || five, six, seven
שָׁלוֹשׁ וְעוֹד אַרְבַּע || three plus four
:::

So a number with no noun behind it is feminine, and a number with a masculine
noun behind it is masculine. The feminine set does double duty; the masculine
set does one job only.

## One, and the word that breaks the pattern

*אֶחָד* and *אַחַת* behave unlike every other number in the language.

::: rule
*אֶחָד* and *אַחַת* come **after** the noun, like an adjective. Every other
number comes before it.
:::

::: examples
תַּלְמִיד אֶחָד || one pupil
תַּלְמִידָה אַחַת || one female pupil
שְׁנֵי תַּלְמִידִים || two pupils
שְׁתֵּי תַּלְמִידוֹת || two female pupils
שְׁלוֹשָׁה תַּלְמִידִים || three pupils
שָׁלוֹשׁ תַּלְמִידוֹת || three female pupils
:::

The reason is that *אֶחָד* is built like an adjective and still behaves like
one. It agrees in gender, it sits behind its noun, and in practice it is often
left out entirely, because an unmarked singular noun already means one of
something.

## Two changes shape

The forms *שְׁנַיִים* and *שְׁתַּיִים* are the counting forms, used when you
say the number on its own. Put a noun after them and they shorten.

::: rule
Before a noun, *שְׁנַיִים* becomes *שְׁנֵי* /shnei/ and *שְׁתַּיִים* becomes
*שְׁתֵּי* /shtei/.
:::

::: examples
שְׁתַּיִים || two, counting
שְׁנֵי סְפָרִים || two books
שְׁתֵּי מַחְבָּרוֹת || two notebooks
שְׁנֵי יְלָדִים גְּדוֹלִים || two big children
:::

Saying *שְׁנַיִים סְפָרִים* is the sort of error that gets corrected on the
spot. No other number does this.

## Eleven to nineteen

Two words: the unit, then the word for ten. The unit keeps its gender, and the
ten-word has a fixed form per gender: *עֶשְׂרֵה* /esre/ with feminine nouns,
*עָשָׂר* /asar/ with masculine ones.

| | Feminine | Masculine |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | אַחַת עֶשְׂרֵה | אַחַד עָשָׂר |
| 12 | שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה | שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר |
| 13 | שְׁלוֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה | שְׁלוֹשָׁה עָשָׂר |
| 14 | אַרְבַּע עֶשְׂרֵה | אַרְבָּעָה עָשָׂר |
| 15 | חֲמֵשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה | חֲמִישָּׁה עָשָׂר |
| 16 | שֵׁשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה | שִׁישָּׁה עָשָׂר |
| 17 | שְׁבַע עֶשְׂרֵה | שִׁבְעָה עָשָׂר |
| 18 | שְׁמוֹנֶה עֶשְׂרֵה | שְׁמוֹנָה עָשָׂר |
| 19 | תְּשַׁע עֶשְׂרֵה | תִּשְׁעָה עָשָׂר |

Note that at 11 and 12 the unit itself changes: *אַחַת* not *אַחַד* on the
feminine side, and *שְׁתֵּים* and *שְׁנֵים* in place of the counting forms.

## The tens

From twenty upwards there is only one form, used for both genders. The tens
are the plural ending *ִים* stuck on the number.

| | Word | | Word |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | עֶשְׂרִים /esrim/ | 30 | שְׁלוֹשִׁים /shloshim/ |
| 40 | אַרְבָּעִים /arba'im/ | 50 | חֲמִישִּׁים /khamishim/ |
| 60 | שִׁישִּׁים /shishim/ | 70 | שִׁבְעִים /shiv'im/ |
| 80 | שְׁמוֹנִים /shmonim/ | 90 | תִּשְׁעִים /tish'im/ |
| 100 | מֵאָה /me'a/ | | |

In between, the ten comes first and the unit follows, joined by *וְ*. The unit
still picks its gender.

::: examples
עֶשְׂרִים וְאַחַת || twenty-one, counting or feminine
עֶשְׂרִים וְאֶחָד || twenty-one, with a masculine noun
שְׁלוֹשִׁים וְשֶׁבַע || thirty-seven
תִּשְׁעִים וְתֵשַׁע || ninety-nine
מֵאָה || a hundred
:::

## Asking how many

*כַּמָּה* /kama/ takes a plural noun straight after it, with no preposition
and no article.

::: examples
כַּמָּה תַּלְמִידִים? || how many pupils?
כַּמָּה מַחְבָּרוֹת? || how many notebooks?
כַּמָּה זֶה? || how much is it?
:::

::: warning
In fast speech many Israelis use the feminine set for everything, masculine
nouns included: *שָׁלוֹשׁ שְׁקָלִים* for *שְׁלוֹשָׁה שְׁקָלִים*. You will hear it
constantly. It is still considered wrong, and a learner who does it sounds
careless rather than native. Learn both sets properly first.
:::

::: exercise Count it
Say "one pupil" (male) || *תַּלְמִיד אֶחָד* || *אֶחָד* follows its noun
Say "two books" || *שְׁנֵי סְפָרִים* || *שְׁנַיִים* shortens to *שְׁנֵי* before a noun
Say "three female pupils" || *שָׁלוֹשׁ תַּלְמִידוֹת* || feminine noun, feminine number, and it comes first
Say "three pupils" (male) || *שְׁלוֹשָׁה תַּלְמִידִים* || the masculine number is the one ending in *ָה*
Count aloud from one to five || *אַחַת, שְׁתַּיִים, שָׁלוֹשׁ, אַרְבַּע, חָמֵשׁ* || counting in the abstract is feminine
Which set do you use for a bus number? || the feminine set || no noun is being counted
Say "fifteen notebooks" || *חֲמֵשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה מַחְבָּרוֹת* || *מַחְבֶּרֶת* is feminine, so *עֶשְׂרֵה*
Ask "how many books?" || *כַּמָּה סְפָרִים?* || *כַּמָּה* takes a bare plural
:::
