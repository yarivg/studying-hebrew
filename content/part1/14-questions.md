# Questions

English builds a question by rearranging the sentence. "You are a student"
becomes "are you a student", and "he lives here" needs a helper verb dragged
in from nowhere: "does he live here". Hebrew does neither. The sentence stays
exactly as it was, and either a question word goes on the front or nothing
changes at all except your voice.

::: rule
A Hebrew question has the same word order as the statement it came from. The
question word goes first; everything after it is untouched.
:::

## The question words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| מַה /ma/ | what |
| מִי /mi/ | who |
| אֵיפֹה /eifo/ | where |
| מָתַי /matai/ | when |
| לָמָּה /lama/ | why |
| מַדּוּעַ /madua'/ | why, formal |
| אֵיךְ /eikh/ | how |
| כַּמָּה /kama/ | how much, how many |
| אֵיזֶה /eize/ | which, masculine |
| אֵיזוֹ /eizo/ | which, feminine |
| לְאָן /le'an/ | where to |
| מֵאַיִן /me'ayin/ | where from |

::: examples
מַה זֶה? || what is this?
מִי זֶה? || who is this?
מִי הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה? || who is this man?
אֵיפֹה הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת? || where is the notebook?
מָתַי הַשִּׁיעוּר? || when is the lesson?
אֵיךְ הָאוֹכֶל? || how is the food?
כַּמָּה זֶה? || how much is it?
מֵאַיִן אַתָּה? || where are you from?
:::

Every one of those is the statement with a word bolted onto the front. There
is no inversion, because there is nothing to invert: the present-tense
sentence has no verb in it to move.

## Which, and the two forms of it

*אֵיזֶה* and *אֵיזוֹ* agree with the noun that follows, the way an adjective
would. Plural is *אֵילוּ* /eilu/, which is rare in speech; most Israelis say
*אֵיזֶה* for everything, including feminine nouns.

::: examples
אֵיזֶה סֵפֶר? || which book?
אֵיזוֹ מַחְבֶּרֶת? || which notebook?
אֵיזֶה יוֹם הַיּוֹם? || what day is it today?
:::

::: note
*אֵיזֶה* also works as an exclamation, and this is extremely common: *אֵיזֶה
יוֹפִי* /eize yofi/, "how lovely", literally "which beauty". Tone tells you
which one you are hearing.
:::

## Two words for why

*לָמָּה* is what people say. *מַדּוּעַ* is what the news reads out and what a
formal letter writes. They mean the same thing, and using *מַדּוּעַ* in a
kitchen conversation sounds like reciting.

## Where to and where from

*אֵיפֹה* asks about a location standing still. *לְאָן* asks about a
destination, and *מֵאַיִן* about an origin. In speech *מֵאַיִן* is mostly
replaced by *מֵאֵיפֹה* /me'eifo/, which every Israeli says and no textbook
prints.

::: examples
אֵיפֹה הַבַּיִת? || where is the house?
לְאָן? || where to?
מֵאַיִן אַתְּ? || where are you from? (to a woman)
:::

::: note
*לְאָן* really wants a verb of motion after it, and the verb is chapter 19.
Register the word now; you will have something to put behind it shortly.
:::

## The yes/no question

Hebrew has no equivalent of "do". To turn a statement into a yes/no question,
you raise your voice at the end and change nothing else.

::: examples
אַתָּה סְטוּדֶנְט. || you are a student.
אַתָּה סְטוּדֶנְט? || are you a student?
הַקָּפֶה חַם? || is the coffee hot?
זֶה הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל? || is this the big house?
:::

::: sound
The rise is on the last syllable, and it is steeper than the English one. A
flat delivery of *אַתָּה סְטוּדֶנְט* will simply be heard as a statement, and
the reply will be a puzzled look rather than an answer.
:::

There is a formal marker, *הַאִם* /ha'im/, which sits at the front and does
the same job in writing.

::: examples
הַאִם אַתָּה סְטוּדֶנְט? || are you a student?
הַאִם זֶה נָכוֹן? || is that correct?
:::

::: warning
*הַאִם* is correct and almost nobody says it. It belongs in a questionnaire, a
contract or a formal speech. In conversation, the intonation alone is the
whole apparatus.
:::

## Answering

*כֵּן* /ken/ is yes and *לֹא* /lo/ is no, and both can stand entirely alone.
Hebrew does not echo the question back the way English does with "yes, I am".

::: examples
אַתָּה סְטוּדֶנְט? כֵּן. || are you a student? Yes.
הַקָּפֶה חַם? לֹא. || is the coffee hot? No.
אֵיפֹה הַסֵּפֶר? בַּבַּיִת. || where is the book? At home.
:::

To an information question you answer with the missing piece and nothing more.
A full sentence in reply is not wrong, it is just more than anyone expects.

::: tip
The single most useful question in the first month is *מַה זֶה?* It converts
anything you can point at into vocabulary, and it takes two syllables.
:::

::: exercise Ask it in Hebrew
Ask "where is the notebook?" || *אֵיפֹה הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת?* || question word first, the rest unchanged
Ask "who is this?" || *מִי זֶה?* || no verb, because the statement has none either
Turn *הַקָּפֶה חַם* into a yes/no question || *הַקָּפֶה חַם?* || nothing changes but the intonation
What is the formal yes/no marker? || *הַאִם* || correct, and rare outside writing
Which is the everyday word for why? || *לָמָּה* || *מַדּוּעַ* is the formal one
Ask "which notebook?" || *אֵיזוֹ מַחְבֶּרֶת?* || *מַחְבֶּרֶת* is feminine, so *אֵיזוֹ*
Ask a woman where she is from || *מֵאַיִן אַתְּ?* || or *מֵאֵיפֹה אַתְּ?* in speech
Why is there no Hebrew word for "do" in a question? || because the question is made by word order and intonation, not by a helper verb || nothing is added and nothing moves
:::
