# The alef-bet

Hebrew has twenty-two letters. All of them are consonants. There are no
capitals, no cursive in print, and the whole thing runs from right to left,
which means the first letter of a word is the one furthest to the right and
the spine of a Hebrew book is on the side English readers reach for last.

Twenty-two is fewer than you are used to, and learning them takes days rather
than weeks. What takes longer is the reflex: reading right to left without
translating the direction in your head first. That comes from doing it, so
this chapter is mostly a table you will come back to.

Five letters have a second shape used only at the end of a word: ך ם ן ף ץ.
They are the same letters, not new ones, and they get a chapter of their own.

::: note
Six letters have two sounds each, decided by a dot inside them: ב, כ and פ
change audibly, and ג, ד and ת once did but no longer do in Israeli Hebrew.
The dot is called a *דָּגֵשׁ* /dagesh/, and it is in chapter 4. Until then,
take the sound the table gives.
:::

## The letters that look alike

This is where every beginner loses time, so learn the pairs now rather than
discovering them later in the middle of a word.

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| ב and כ | ב has a corner at the bottom right, כ is rounded there |
| ד and ר | ד has a heel sticking out at the top right, ר is smooth |
| ה and ח | ה has a gap at the top left, ח is closed |
| ה and ת | ת has a foot at the bottom left, ה has a floating leg |
| ו and ז | ז has a bar across the top, ו is a plain stroke |
| ו and ן | ן drops below the line, ו sits on it |
| ס and ם | ם has square corners, ס is round |
| ע and צ | ע opens upwards into a V, צ has a stroke crossing it |
| כ and ל | ל rises above the line, כ does not |

::: tip
A quick drill: cover the names in the table above, read down the letter
column, and say the sound. Then do the same reading up. Ten minutes a day for
a week is enough, and it is the only rote learning this course asks for.
:::

## Two letters that make no sound

*אָלֶף* and *עַיִן* are consonants with nothing to hear. In Biblical Hebrew and
in the speech of many Israelis whose families came from Arabic-speaking
countries they are distinct sounds, made in the throat. In standard Israeli
Hebrew today neither is pronounced at all: they are silent seats that a vowel
sits on.

That matters for spelling, not for speaking. *אוֹר* /or/ light and *עוֹר* /or/
skin sound identical and are different words. You cannot hear which letter to
write, so you learn it with the word.

::: examples
אוֹר || light
עוֹר || skin, leather
אִישׁ || a man
עִיר || a city
:::

## What is missing

There are no vowel letters in that table. Hebrew writes the consonants and
leaves the vowels to be supplied, either by a system of dots called *נִקּוּד*
/nikud/, which this course uses throughout, or by the reader, which is what
happens in every newspaper, street sign and text message in Israel.

Both are coming: the points in chapter 3, reading without them in chapter 7.
For now, notice what it means. *ספר* is three letters and could be *סֵפֶר* a
book, *סַפָּר* a barber, or *סִפֵּר* he told. They are all the same root doing
different jobs, which is the single most important fact about the language and
the reason a Hebrew dictionary is organised by root rather than by spelling.

::: rule
A Hebrew word is built from a root of consonants, usually three, poured into a
pattern. The root carries the meaning, the pattern carries the grammar. Once
you can see both at once, most of the vocabulary stops needing to be memorised
one word at a time.
:::

::: exercise Read the letters
Which letter is this: ד || *דָּלֶת* /dalet/ || the heel at the top right is the give-away; ר has none
Which letter is this: ם || *מֵם* /mem/, final form || square corners; ס is round
Which two letters are silent? || *אָלֶף* and *עַיִן* || they carry a vowel but have no sound of their own
Say the sound of ח || kh, made in the throat || the same sound as כ without its dot, and nothing like English h
How many letters are there in the alef-bet? || twenty-two || plus five final forms, which are not extra letters
Write the first word of *שָׁלוֹם* letter by letter, right to left || ש, ל, ו, ם || the ם is final because it ends the word
:::
