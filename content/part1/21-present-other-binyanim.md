# Present tense: pi'el, hif'il, hitpa'el

Every verb so far has been in one pattern, *פָּעַל* /pa'al/. Hebrew has seven
such patterns, and a root can be poured into several of them to give several
related verbs. A pattern is called a *בִּנְיָין* /binyan/, literally a
"building", plural *בִּנְיָינִים* /binyanim/.

::: rule
The root carries the meaning. The binyan carries what is being done with that
meaning: doing it, causing it, doing it to yourself. Same three letters, a
different mould, a different verb.
:::

This chapter does not teach the whole system, which waits for Part II. It
teaches you to recognise three more binyanim in the present tense, because
between them and pa'al you have almost everything a beginner says.

## The מ that gives it away

Start with the single most useful reading clue in Hebrew.

::: rule
Every present-tense verb outside pa'al begins with *מ*. If a word starts with
*מ* and is not obviously a noun, suspect a verb.
:::

*מְדַבֵּר*, *מַסְבִּיר*, *מִתְלַבֵּשׁ*: all present tense, all binyanim other
than pa'al. Pa'al does not do this, which is why *כּוֹתֵב* and *רוֹצֶה* have no
*מ*. Once you can see the *מ* you can pick verbs out of unpointed text
immediately, which is most of the battle.

## פִּיעֵל: מְ plus a doubled middle letter

*פִּיעֵל* /pi'el/ puts *מְ* on the front, an a vowel after it, and a dagesh in
the middle root letter.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *מְדַבֵּר* | *מְדַבְּרִים* |
| feminine | *מְדַבֶּרֶת* | *מְדַבְּרוֹת* |

*מְדַבֵּר* /medaber/, to speak, infinitive *לְדַבֵּר*. The feminine singular
takes *ֶת*, exactly as a pa'al sound root does.

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לְבַשֵּׁל* | מְבַשֵּׁל | מְבַשֶּׁלֶת | מְבַשְּׁלִים | מְבַשְּׁלוֹת | cook |
| *לְסַפֵּר* | מְסַפֵּר | מְסַפֶּרֶת | מְסַפְּרִים | מְסַפְּרוֹת | tell, narrate |
| *לְשַׁלֵּם* | מְשַׁלֵּם | מְשַׁלֶּמֶת | מְשַׁלְּמִים | מְשַׁלְּמוֹת | pay |

::: examples
אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר עִבְרִית || I speak Hebrew
הִיא מְבַשֶּׁלֶת מָרָק || she is cooking soup
הֵם מְשַׁלְּמִים בְּכַרְטִיס || they pay by card
סַבְתָּא מְסַפֶּרֶת סִיפּוּר || grandma is telling a story
:::

Note the infinitive: pi'el infinitives start *לְ* with a shva, not *לִ*.

## הִפְעִיל: מַ plus an i vowel

*הִפְעִיל* /hif'il/ puts *מַ* with a patach on the front, and an i vowel, a
chirik, before the last root letter.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *מַסְבִּיר* | *מַסְבִּירִים* |
| feminine | *מַסְבִּירָה* | *מַסְבִּירוֹת* |

*מַסְבִּיר* /masbir/, to explain, infinitive *לְהַסְבִּיר*.

::: warning
Hif'il is the one family whose feminine singular is *ָה* and not *ֶת*:
*מַסְבִּירָה*, never *מַסְבִּירֶת*. It is the easiest of the four endings to
get wrong, because pi'el and hitpa'el on either side of it both take *ֶת*.
:::

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לְהַתְחִיל* | מַתְחִיל | מַתְחִילָה | מַתְחִילִים | מַתְחִילוֹת | begin |
| *לְהַרְגִּישׁ* | מַרְגִּישׁ | מַרְגִּישָׁה | מַרְגִּישִׁים | מַרְגִּישׁוֹת | feel |
| *לְהָבִין* | מֵבִין | מְבִינָה | מְבִינִים | מְבִינוֹת | understand |

::: note
*לְהָבִין* is hif'il built on a root whose middle letter is *י*, so the *מַ*
collapses to *מֵ* and the word comes out /mevin/, two syllables with nothing
between them. It is among the twenty most used verbs in the language, so learn
it as a word and do not try to derive it.
:::

::: examples
הוּא מַסְבִּיר אֶת הַשִּׁיעוּר || he is explaining the lesson
אֲנִי מֵבִין עִבְרִית || I understand Hebrew
הַסֶּרֶט מַתְחִיל בְּשֶׁבַע || the film starts at seven
אֵיךְ אַתְּ מַרְגִּישָׁה? || how do you feel?
:::

## הִתְפַּעֵל: מִתְ on the front

*הִתְפַּעֵל* /hitpa'el/ is the easiest of all to spot: the whole word starts
with *מִתְ*.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *מִתְלַבֵּשׁ* | *מִתְלַבְּשִׁים* |
| feminine | *מִתְלַבֶּשֶׁת* | *מִתְלַבְּשׁוֹת* |

*מִתְלַבֵּשׁ* /mitlabesh/, to get dressed, infinitive *לְהִתְלַבֵּשׁ*. After the
*מִתְ* the word behaves like pi'el, endings included.

| Infinitive | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *לְהִתְרַחֵץ* | מִתְרַחֵץ | מִתְרַחֶצֶת | מִתְרַחֲצִים | מִתְרַחֲצוֹת | wash, shower |
| *לְהִתְקַשֵּׁר* | מִתְקַשֵּׁר | מִתְקַשֶּׁרֶת | מִתְקַשְּׁרִים | מִתְקַשְּׁרוֹת | phone, call |

::: examples
הַיֶּלֶד מִתְלַבֵּשׁ לְבַד || the child dresses himself
אֲנִי מִתְרַחֵץ בַּבּוֹקֶר || I shower in the morning
הִיא מִתְקַשֶּׁרֶת לְאִימָּא || she is calling mum
:::

## Telling them apart

::: rule
*מְ* plus a dagesh in the middle letter: pi'el. *מַ* plus an i vowel before
the last letter: hif'il. *מִתְ*: hitpa'el. No *מ* at all: pa'al.
:::

| Binyan | Looks like | Feminine singular | Infinitive starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| pa'al | *כּוֹתֵב* | *ֶת* | *לִ* or *לָ* |
| pi'el | *מְדַבֵּר* | *ֶת* | *לְ* |
| hif'il | *מַסְבִּיר* | *ָה* | *לְהַ* |
| hitpa'el | *מִתְלַבֵּשׁ* | *ֶת* | *לְהִתְ* |

## What each one tends to mean

Pi'el often intensifies or makes an action deliberate and transitive. Hif'il is
the causative: it makes someone else do the thing, or makes it happen.
Hitpa'el is reflexive, done to yourself, or reciprocal, done to each other.

::: examples
הוּא מִתְלַבֵּשׁ || he dresses himself: hitpa'el, reflexive
הִיא מַלְבִּישָׁה אֶת הַיֶּלֶד || she dresses the child: hif'il, causative
הֵם מִתְכַּתְּבִים || they write to each other: hitpa'el, reciprocal
:::

::: warning
Treat these meanings as tendencies, not as rules you can generate new verbs
from. *מְדַבֵּר* is pi'el and nothing about speaking is intensive.
*מִתְקַשֵּׁר*, "phones", is hitpa'el and is not reflexive at all. Use the
pattern to recognise the shape of a word and to guess its binyan; learn its
meaning from the dictionary.
:::

::: exercise Spot the binyan
Which binyan is *מַתְחִיל*? || hif'il || *מַ* with a patach, then an i vowel
Which binyan is *מִתְרַחֵץ*? || hitpa'el || the *מִתְ* prefix
Make *מַסְבִּיר* feminine singular || *מַסְבִּירָה* || hif'il takes *ָה*, not *ֶת*
Make *מְדַבֵּר* feminine singular || *מְדַבֶּרֶת* || pi'el takes *ֶת*
Say "they are cooking" (women) || *הֵן מְבַשְּׁלוֹת* || from *לְבַשֵּׁל*
What does a *מ* at the start of a verb tell you? || that it is present tense and not pa'al || pa'al present has no prefix
Give the infinitive of *מְשַׁלֵּם* || *לְשַׁלֵּם* || pi'el infinitives start with *לְ*
Say "I am getting dressed" (a woman) || *אֲנִי מִתְלַבֶּשֶׁת* || the pronoun is still compulsory
:::
