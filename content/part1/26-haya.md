# Was and will be

Chapter 11 made a point of it: the present tense has no verb "to be".
*אֲנִי סְטוּדֶנְט* is a finished sentence, and putting a word for "am" into it
would be an error. That is true, and it is only true in the present. Move the
same sentence to yesterday or to next week and Hebrew suddenly needs a verb,
and it is this one.

::: rule
There is no "is". There is very much a "was" and a "will be". The verb
*לִהְיוֹת* /lihyot/ is absent in the present and compulsory everywhere else.
:::

## The past

This is the first full past tense in the course, so read it as a table rather
than as a system. The system is Part II chapter 1.

| | |
|---|---|
| *אֲנִי הָיִיתִי* | I was |
| *אַתָּה הָיִיתָ* | you (m. sg.) were |
| *אַתְּ הָיִית* | you (f. sg.) were |
| *הוּא הָיָה* | he was |
| *הִיא הָיְתָה* | she was |
| *אֲנַחְנוּ הָיִינוּ* | we were |
| *אַתֶּם הֱיִיתֶם* | you (m. pl.) were |
| *אַתֶּן הֱיִיתֶן* | you (f. pl.) were |
| *הֵם, הֵן הָיוּ* | they were |

*הָיָה* /haya/ is the dictionary form. Note that the past distinguishes
person, which the present never did: *הָיִיתִי* /hayiti/ can only mean "I was",
so the pronoun in front of it is optional and often dropped.

::: sound
*הָיִיתָ* and *הָיִית* are pronounced /hayita/ and /hayit/, and in fast speech
the second sounds like the first with the ending bitten off. *הֱיִיתֶם* is the
Academy's form; in speech it is levelled to /hayitem/, opening with the
same vowel as the rest of the table.
:::

## The future, for recognition only

You will hear these long before Part II teaches you to build them, so learn
them as eight words.

| | |
|---|---|
| *אֶהְיֶה* | I will be |
| *תִּהְיֶה* | you (m. sg.) will be |
| *תִּהְיִי* | you (f. sg.) will be |
| *יִהְיֶה* | he, it will be |
| *תִּהְיֶה* | she will be |
| *נִהְיֶה* | we will be |
| *תִּהְיוּ* | you (pl.) will be |
| *יִהְיוּ* | they will be |

The full future, with its prefixes and what they mean, is Part II chapter 3.
Until then, one phrase is worth having whole: *יִהְיֶה בְּסֵדֶר*, it'll be
fine, which is said in Israel roughly every four seconds.

## Having, in the past and the future

Chapter 16 gave you *יֵשׁ לִי* and *אֵין לִי*, and pointed out that Hebrew has
no verb "to have". It still has none. What it has is *הָיָה* and *יִהְיֶה*
standing in for *יֵשׁ*.

::: examples
יֵשׁ לִי זְמַן || I have time
הָיָה לִי זְמַן || I had time
יִהְיֶה לִי זְמַן || I'll have time
אֵין לִי זְמַן || I don't have time
לֹא הָיָה לִי זְמַן || I didn't have time
לֹא יִהְיֶה לִי זְמַן || I won't have time
:::

Note that the negative in the past and the future is plain *לֹא*, not *אֵין*.
*אֵין* only does the present.

::: warning
The verb agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner. The owner is buried
inside *לִי*, where no verb can reach it.
:::

::: examples
הָיוּ לִי סְפָרִים || I had books
הָיְתָה לִי מְכוֹנִית || I had a car
הָיָה לוֹ כֶּלֶב || he had a dog
יִהְיוּ לָנוּ שְׁאֵלוֹת || we'll have questions
:::

A woman says *הָיָה לִי זְמַן* exactly as a man does, because *זְמַן* is what
the verb is agreeing with. This catches every English speaker at least once.

## The past of a sentence with no verb

Take any of the verbless sentences from chapters 11 and 12, and put *הָיָה* in
where the missing "is" would have gone. It agrees with the subject.

::: examples
הַבַּיִת גָּדוֹל || the house is big
הַבַּיִת הָיָה גָּדוֹל || the house was big
אֲנִי סְטוּדֶנְט || I am a student
הָיִיתִי סְטוּדֶנְט || I was a student
הִיא עֲיֵיפָה || she is tired
הִיא הָיְתָה עֲיֵיפָה || she was tired
הֵם בַּבַּיִת || they are at home
הֵם הָיוּ בַּבַּיִת || they were at home
:::

## There was, there was not

*הָיָה* also covers the existential *יֵשׁ*, and the same agreement rule
applies: the verb matches whatever is being said to exist.

::: examples
הָיָה חַם אֶתְמוֹל || it was hot yesterday
הָיְתָה מְסִיבָּה || there was a party
הָיוּ הַרְבֵּה אֲנָשִׁים || there were a lot of people
לֹא הָיָה אוֹכֶל || there was no food
:::

::: note
*הָיָה* plus a present-tense verb means "used to": *הָיִיתִי גָּר בְּחֵיפָה*,
I used to live in Haifa. You will meet it properly in Part II; recognise it
now so it does not read as a mistake.
:::

And the infinitive, from the chapter before this one, behaves like any other:
*אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לִהְיוֹת רוֹפֵא*, I want to be a doctor. That infinitive is the
one place the verb "to be" shows up while you are still talking about now.

::: exercise Move the sentence in time
Say "I was at home" || *הָיִיתִי בַּבַּיִת* || the pronoun is optional
Say "she was tired" || *הִיא הָיְתָה עֲיֵיפָה* || *הָיְתָה* for a feminine subject
Put *הַבַּיִת גָּדוֹל* into the past || *הַבַּיִת הָיָה גָּדוֹל* || the missing "is" becomes a real verb
Say "I had a car" || *הָיְתָה לִי מְכוֹנִית* || agrees with *מְכוֹנִית*, not with you
Say "I had books" || *הָיוּ לִי סְפָרִים* || plural thing owned, plural verb
Say "I didn't have time" || *לֹא הָיָה לִי זְמַן* || *לֹא*, not *אֵין*, once you leave the present
Say "it'll be fine" || *יִהְיֶה בְּסֵדֶר* || the future of *הָיָה* for a masculine subject
What is wrong with *אֲנִי הָיָה סְטוּדֶנְט*? || it must agree with *אֲנִי*: *הָיִיתִי סְטוּדֶנְט* || *הָיָה* is third person
:::
