# This and that

Hebrew has one set of words for "this" and "these", and it uses the same words
for "that" and "those". There is no built-in near-far distinction of the kind
English has; when it matters, you add a word.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *זֶה* /ze/ | *אֵלֶּה* /ele/ |
| feminine | *זֹאת* /zot/ | *אֵלֶּה* |

::: note
*אֵלֶּה* covers both genders in the plural. In speech many Israelis say
*אֵלּוּ* /elu/, which is equally correct and slightly more formal in writing.
*זֹאת* also has a common variant *זוֹ* /zo/.
:::

## Standing alone

Used by itself, the demonstrative is the subject of a verbless sentence, and
it is how you point at something and ask or say what it is.

::: examples
מָה זֶה? || what is this?
זֶה סֵפֶר || this is a book
זֹאת מַחְבֶּרֶת || this is a notebook
אֵלֶּה סְפָרִים || these are books
מִי זֶה? || who is this?
זֶה דָּנִי || this is Dani
:::

::: rule
The demonstrative agrees with what is being identified, not with anything
else. *זֶה סֵפֶר* because *סֵפֶר* is masculine; *זֹאת מַחְבֶּרֶת* because
*מַחְבֶּרֶת* is feminine.
:::

::: warning
When you do not yet know what the thing is, use *זֶה*. *מָה זֶה?* is the
question for a book, a notebook, a smell or an unidentified object. The
masculine is the default, and you only switch to *זֹאת* once you know the
answer is feminine.
:::

## Attached to a noun

This is where Hebrew differs from English, and it follows straight from
chapter 12. Used as a modifier, the demonstrative behaves exactly like an
adjective: it comes **after** the noun, and it takes the article when the noun
has one.

::: examples
הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה || this book
הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת הַזֹּאת || this notebook
הַיְלָדִים הָאֵלֶּה || these children
הַבָּתִּים הָאֵלֶּה || these houses
:::

::: rule
"This book" is *הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה*, literally "the book the this". Both words
carry the article. There is no way to say "this book" with only one *הַ*.
:::

And the contrast from chapter 12 works here too:

| With two articles | With one |
|---|---|
| *הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה* this book | *הַסֵּפֶר זֶה* is not Hebrew |
| *הַיְלָדִים הָאֵלֶּה* these children | *זֶה הַסֵּפֶר* this is the book |

*זֶה הַסֵּפֶר* with the demonstrative in front is a sentence: "this is the
book". Word order carries the difference.

::: examples
זֶה הַסֵּפֶר || this is the book
הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה || this book
זֹאת הַדִּירָה || this is the flat
הַדִּירָה הַזֹּאת || this flat
:::

## With an adjective as well

The demonstrative goes last.

::: examples
הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה || this big house
הַיַּלְדָּה הַקְּטַנָּה הַזֹּאת || this little girl
הַסְּפָרִים הַחֲדָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה || these new books
:::

Three articles in a row looks alarming and is completely ordinary.

## That, over there

When the near-far contrast matters, Hebrew adds a word rather than changing
the demonstrative.

::: examples
הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה || this book
הַסֵּפֶר הַהוּא || that book
הַדִּירָה הַהִיא || that flat
הַסֵּפֶר שָׁם || the book over there
:::

*הַהוּא* and *הַהִיא* are the third-person pronouns doing double duty, and they
are noticeably formal. In speech most Israelis say *הַזֶּה* and point, or add
*שָׁם* /sham/, there.

::: tip
Do not go looking for a word for "that". In nine sentences out of ten,
Hebrew's *זֶה* is the right translation of both "this" and "that", and adding
*הַהוּא* makes you sound like a textbook.
:::

## The phrases built on it

::: examples
מָה זֶה? || what is this?
זֶה בְּסֵדֶר || that's fine
זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן || that's not true
זֶה הַכֹּל || that's all
בִּגְלַל זֶה || because of that
אַחֲרֵי זֶה || after this
זֶה לֹא מְעַנְיֵין || it's not interesting
:::

Notice the last one: *זֶה* is doing the work of English "it" in a sentence
with no particular thing in mind. That is its most frequent job of all.

::: exercise This and that
Say "this is a book" || *זֶה סֵפֶר* || the demonstrative stands in front and there is no verb
Say "this book" || *הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה* || after the noun, and both words take the article
Say "this is the book" || *זֶה הַסֵּפֶר* || demonstrative first makes it a sentence
Say "this notebook" || *הַמַּחְבֶּרֶת הַזֹּאת* || *מַחְבֶּרֶת* is feminine, so *זֹאת*
Say "these children" || *הַיְלָדִים הָאֵלֶּה* || *אֵלֶּה* covers both genders
Someone shows you an object you cannot identify. What do you ask? || *מָה זֶה?* || the masculine is the default when you do not know
Say "this big house" || *הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה* || the demonstrative comes after the adjective
How would you say "that book", and would an Israeli? || *הַסֵּפֶר הַהוּא*, and mostly not || in speech it is *הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה* with a gesture, or *שָׁם*
:::
