# Adjectives

A Hebrew adjective comes after its noun and agrees with it in three things:
gender, number, and definiteness. The first two will be familiar from any
European language. The third is the one that makes Hebrew sentences work, and
it has no equivalent in English.

## The four forms

Every adjective has four: masculine singular, feminine singular, masculine
plural, feminine plural. The endings are the same ones nouns use.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | *גָּדוֹל* | *גְּדוֹלִים* |
| feminine | *גְּדוֹלָה* | *גְּדוֹלוֹת* |

::: examples
בַּיִת גָּדוֹל || a big house
דִּירָה גְּדוֹלָה || a big flat
בָּתִּים גְּדוֹלִים || big houses
דִּירוֹת גְּדוֹלוֹת || big flats
:::

The dictionary form is the masculine singular, and this course writes an
adjective as masculine slash feminine: *גָּדוֹל / גְּדוֹלָה*.

## Agreement with the noun, not the ending

Agreement follows the noun's actual gender, which is not always what its
ending suggests. This is where the irregular plurals of chapter 9 come back to
collect.

::: examples
נָשִׁים יָפוֹת || beautiful women: *נָשִׁים* is feminine despite the *ִים*
שׁוּלְחָנוֹת גְּדוֹלִים || big tables: *שׁוּלְחָנוֹת* is masculine despite the *וֹת*
עָרִים יָפוֹת || beautiful cities: *עִיר* is feminine
מְקוֹמוֹת חֲדָשִׁים || new places: *מָקוֹם* is masculine
:::

::: warning
*נָשִׁים יָפִים* is wrong and unmistakably foreign. If you learn one pair of
words in this chapter, learn *נָשִׁים יָפוֹת*.
:::

## The four shapes

Most adjectives follow one of a few patterns, and knowing them lets you form
the feminine of a word you have only met in the masculine.

**1. Add *ָה*.** The default.

::: examples
גָּדוֹל becomes גְּדוֹלָה || big
טוֹב becomes טוֹבָה || good
חָדָשׁ becomes חֲדָשָׁה || new
יָשָׁן becomes יְשָׁנָה || old
:::

**2. Adjectives in *ֶה* replace it with *ָה*.**

::: examples
יָפֶה becomes יָפָה || beautiful
קָשֶׁה becomes קָשָׁה || difficult
שׁוֹנֶה becomes שׁוֹנָה || different
:::

**3. Present-tense verb forms used as adjectives take *ֶת*.**

::: examples
מְעַנְיֵין becomes מְעַנְיֶינֶת || interesting
מְלוּכְלָךְ becomes מְלוּכְלֶכֶת || dirty
:::

**4. A small group doubles a letter.**

::: examples
קָטָן becomes קְטַנָּה || small
:::

::: note
*יָפֶה* and *יָפָה* differ only in one vowel sign, and without nikud they are
both יפה. Israelis tell them apart from the noun. You will too.
:::

## The third agreement: definiteness

::: rule
If the noun has *הַ*, the adjective must have *הַ* too. If the noun does not,
the adjective must not.
:::

::: examples
בַּיִת גָּדוֹל || a big house
הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל || the big house
יְלָדִים קְטַנִּים || small children
הַיְלָדִים הַקְּטַנִּים || the small children
:::

Both words carry the article, or neither does. There is no middle.

And the middle, when it happens, means something else entirely:

::: examples
הַבַּיִת גָּדוֹל || the house is big
הַיְלָדִים קְטַנִּים || the children are small
הַקָּפֶה חַם || the coffee is hot
הַשִּׁיעוּר מְעַנְיֵין || the lesson is interesting
:::

::: rule
Definite noun plus indefinite adjective is a sentence, not a phrase. The
adjective cannot be describing the noun, because it does not agree in
definiteness, so it must be saying something about it.
:::

This is the most important single pattern in beginner's Hebrew, and it is
worth saying out loud a few times:

| Phrase | Sentence |
|---|---|
| *הַבַּיִת הַגָּדוֹל* the big house | *הַבַּיִת גָּדוֹל* the house is big |
| *הַסֵּפֶר הֶחָדָשׁ* the new book | *הַסֵּפֶר חָדָשׁ* the book is new |
| *הַיַּלְדָּה הַקְּטַנָּה* the little girl | *הַיַּלְדָּה קְטַנָּה* the girl is little |

An indefinite noun cannot pull the same trick, because nothing distinguishes
the two readings. *בַּיִת גָּדוֹל* is "a big house" and, in the right context,
"a house is big". Hebrew does not worry about it.

## More than one adjective

They queue up after the noun, each agreeing separately, joined by *וְ* or not.

::: examples
בַּיִת גָּדוֹל וְיָשָׁן || a big old house
הַדִּירָה הַחֲדָשָׁה הַיָּפָה || the beautiful new flat
יְלָדִים קְטַנִּים וַחֲמוּדִים || small, sweet children
:::

Hebrew has no rule about adjective order of the kind English has. Put the more
important one first.

## Very, quite, too

These come after the adjective, not before it.

::: examples
גָּדוֹל מְאוֹד || very big
קָטָן מִדַּי || too small
יָפֶה מְאוֹד || very beautiful
קְצָת יָקָר || a little expensive
:::

*מְאוֹד* and *מִדַּי* follow. *קְצָת* leads. Adjectives do not change their
form for any of them.

::: exercise Agree with the noun
Say "a big flat" || *דִּירָה גְּדוֹלָה* || *דִּירָה* is feminine, so the adjective is too
Say "the big flat" || *הַדִּירָה הַגְּדוֹלָה* || both words take the article
Say "the flat is big" || *הַדִּירָה גְּדוֹלָה* || only the noun takes it, which turns the phrase into a sentence
Say "beautiful women" || *נָשִׁים יָפוֹת* || *נָשִׁים* is feminine, whatever its ending looks like
Say "big tables" || *שׁוּלְחָנוֹת גְּדוֹלִים* || *שׁוּלְחָן* is masculine, whatever its plural looks like
Make *יָפֶה* feminine || *יָפָה* || adjectives ending in *ֶה* swap it for *ָה*
Make *קָטָן* feminine || *קְטַנָּה* || the נ doubles
Say "very interesting" || *מְעַנְיֵין מְאוֹד* || *מְאוֹד* comes after the adjective
:::
